# Story Dice Studio

*Roll a hero, a place, and a problem — then tell a story that's never existed before.*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 6–12 · **Chinese name:** 故事骰子工作室 · **Spec version:** 1.1

## What it is

Nine dice — 3 Hero, 3 Place, 3 Problem, 300+ faces — start every story. The 5-step Storyteller's Map (Opening, Big Trouble, Clever Idea, Twist, Ending) gives the tale its shape, and the coach, Professor Plot the owl, only ever asks questions — the story stays the child's. Three creation modes: Tell It (voice), Write It, or Draw It (4-panel comic). Finished stories collect in the Studio Book, and a weekly Impossible Roll dares young authors to combine the strangest roll of all.

## Features

- 9 dice, 300+ faces: Hero / Place / Problem
- 5-step Storyteller's Map gives every tale a shape
- Coach Professor Plot only asks questions
- Tell It (voice) / Write It / Draw It (4-panel comic)
- Studio Book of finished stories + weekly Impossible Roll
- One Wild Face die: a tenth die whose face the author may declare to be anything — it teaches that a storyteller may bend one rule on purpose, and say out loud which rule they bent
- Second Chance token: one re-roll per story, spent deliberately — re-rolling is framed as an author's revision choice, never as fixing a "bad" roll, and the spent token is shown on the finished story's page
- A check-in question at every Map step: Professor Plot asks exactly one question per step (never two, never an answer), so a stuck author is rescued by a question, not handed a plot
- Stretch word: one grown-up word per story, woven in by the coach with a kid-sized definition and a challenge to use it again next time — the word is recorded on the story's Studio Book page
- Mid-step rescue: if an author stalls, the coach offers three opening phrases for that step — the author picks one or ignores all three; the story is never auto-written
- Finished stories can be read aloud back to the author in Tell It mode, and Studio Book pages print cleanly for a real-world shelf copy
- Forge standards: gentle music-box music with big stop button, silent parent-only play timing

## Sample content

### Sample roll → Captain Bea and the Library That Flew Away

The roll: Hero — Captain Bea, a shy octopus inventor. Place — the Floating Library. Problem — the anchor rope has snapped.

The finished story: Captain Bea had invented many quiet things — a kettle that whispered, boots for puddles — but she had never invented anything brave. The morning the Floating Library's anchor rope snapped, the whole library began to drift toward the cloud-sea, with every book fluttering like a startled bird. Bea's eight arms shook. Then she remembered: a library is just ideas holding hands. She tied the longest bookmarks together, arm over arm over arm, and made a rope of stories — adventure to atlas, poem to pirate tale. The library held. That night Bea wrote a new label for her toolbox: “Bravery: inventing the thing you need, with hands still shaking.” Professor Plot's only comment: “What will Bea dare to invent next?”

### Sample Impossible Roll → The Night Train That Lost Its Stations (with Wild Face and stretch word)

The Impossible Roll: Hero — a retired lighthouse keeper named Marta. Place — a night train. Problem — every station sign has gone blank. Wild Face declared by the author: "The train's whistle can speak, but only in questions." Stretch word: *vanish* — to disappear completely, all at once.

The finished story: Marta had kept a lighthouse for forty years, so she knew what a light was for: it tells you where you are. The night train's stations had vanished — not the stations themselves, just their names, every sign wiped white as the train rolled into the dark. The whistle cleared its throat and asked, “Where would you stop, if stopping were yours to choose?” Marta did what keepers do. At each silent platform she leaned out, listened, and named what she could hear: gulls and a fish market here — that is Gullwick. A bakery shutter and warm cinnamon there — Crumbton. A hundred frogs in a pond — Little Puddle, surely. The passengers wrote her names on the blank signs in chalk, laughing, and the whistle asked its last question of the night: “Who keeps the light now, keeper?” Marta hung her lantern on the train's front hook and rode home sitting taller than she had in years. Professor Plot's only comment: “Which station would you name first — and what would you listen for?” Studio Book record: Second Chance token — not spent. Stretch word used: vanish.
