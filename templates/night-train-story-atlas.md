# The Night Train Story Atlas

*A cozy station-by-station story journey*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 5–9 · **Chinese name:** 夜行火车故事地图 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

Ride a gentle night train where every station opens a tiny story. Your child chooses who boards, what the station holds, and how the journey bends — Muse narrates one calm scene at a time, repeats your child's ideas back, and stamps the route atlas at each stop. No scores, no rushing, just a cozy journey that ends exactly how you choose: home by bedtime, asleep on the train, or arriving at sunrise.

## Features

- One calm scene at a time — Muse narrates, your child steers the journey
- Every station is a tiny complete story: one choice, one line added by your child, confirmed back
- Route atlas stamped at every stop — the journey grows night by night
- Three gentle endings, chosen by you: home by bedtime, asleep on the train, or sunrise arrival
- No scores, no timers, no rushing — a cozy ride at your child's pace

## What's included

- Route atlas with station stamps

## At a glance

- Board at the Moon Platform — the owl conductor punches a golden ticket under a crescent moon
- Every station is a tiny story — a lighthouse kitchen, a snow-drift library, a desert star market, each in warm amber and rose
- Stamp the atlas at every stop — the route fills in as the journey grows, ready to continue tomorrow night

## How it works

1. Pick a route theme and crew, and board at the Moon Platform
2. Visit three stations in one session — at each station the child makes one choice and adds one line, and Muse continues the scene
3. Ride to the chosen ending and stamp the atlas

## Sample content

### Sample · Night One: The Coast of Small Lights (a complete three-station journey)

SESSION CARD — Traveler: Alex · Ages 7–8 · English · About 15 min · Route: Moonlit coast · Crew: Mina the owl conductor + Bruno the turtle engineer · Pace: Gentle & slow · Ending: Home by bedtime.

BOARDING — THE MOON PLATFORM

NARRATOR (slowly): The Moon Platform appears only when the sky is properly dark and the street lamps have come on. It has one bench, one clock that ticks very softly, and a crescent moon hanging low enough to read by.

Mina the owl conductor steps down from the train in her navy cap. “Ticket, please,” she says — not stern, just warm, the way librarians ask.

ALEX'S TURN: Alex holds out the golden ticket with both hands.

NARRATOR: Mina punches it — click — and a tiny crescent-shaped hole appears in the corner. “One crescent for the first night,” she says. “Three stations tonight, and the atlas gets its first stamp. All aboard, Alex. Mind the warm step.”

STATION 1 — THE LIGHTHOUSE KITCHEN

NARRATOR: The first station is built into the bottom of a lighthouse, and it smells of warm bread and sea salt. The lighthouse keeper is standing on a stool, frowning up at the great lamp, which is flickering like a candle in a draft.

KEEPER: “The light has lost its lullaby. Every night I hum it the kettle song while it warms up — kettle first, then light. Tonight I have forgotten the tune, and the light will not settle.”

NARRATOR: Mina looks at Alex. Conductors know when a journey needs its traveler.

ALEX'S LINE: “Hum the kettle song — the one that goes up at the end, like steam.”

NARRATOR (repeats it back, so it becomes true): “You said: hum the kettle song, the one that goes up at the end like steam. Yes — that is exactly what the keeper does.”

The keeper hums — low, then rising, then low again — and one by one the flickers smooth out, until the beam turns steady and slow over the water, the way it is supposed to. The keeper climbs down, wipes flour on his apron, and bows to Alex. “The light is calm because of you. Now — a traveler who fixes a light must be fed.”

YOUR CHOICE, ALEX (the story pauses here, and waits): What does the keeper serve — a cup of moon-milk, warm and honey-sweet, or a slice of star toast, buttered and sparkling at the edges? Whichever you choose, the keeper serves it on the good plate, and the story continues from your plate.

STATION 2 — THE TIDEPOOL BRIDGE

NARRATOR: Past the lighthouse, the track runs out over the sea on a long, low bridge, and the water on both sides is smooth as glass. Bruno the turtle engineer leans out of the engine, slow and pleased. Bruno counts everything on this bridge. It is his favorite job.

BRUNO (very slowly, which is how turtles count best): “One silver fish… two silver fish… three silver fish. Three. And every one of them glowing.”

NARRATOR: It is true. Below the bridge, three silver fish are drifting in a line, each one shining with a soft green-gold light, lit from inside like tiny lanterns. Mina slows the train to a walking pace so nobody misses it.

MINA: “In all my years on this line, no one has ever told me why they glow. The atlas page is blank, Alex. It is waiting for the traveler.”

ALEX'S LINE: “They carry tiny lanterns for the baby crabs, so the baby crabs can find their way home in the dark.”

NARRATOR (repeats it back, and writes it in the atlas in gold ink): “Then it is settled, and from tonight it is true on this whole line: the silver fish carry tiny lanterns for the baby crabs, so the baby crabs can always find their way home. Bruno, did you hear?”

BRUNO: “I heard. Three fish. Three lanterns. No baby crab lost. Not on my bridge.”

STATION 3 — THE VILLAGE OF SMALL LIGHTS

NARRATOR: The last station is not really a station — it is a whole village of small houses along the shore, and as the train comes around the curve, something wonderful happens. One window lights up. Then another. Then another, and another, all down the street, gold square after gold square, until the whole village is glowing.

MINA (delighted, adjusting her cap): “Do you hear that, Alex? Under the sound of the wheels — the kettle song. The keeper hummed it up at the lighthouse, and the light carried it over the water, and the village heard it. They are lighting their windows to say: we heard the lullaby, and we are glad the light is steady tonight.”

ALEX'S TURN: Alex leans out and waves at every window, one by one, all the way down the street. Every window waves back — curtains twitch, lamps bob, one teapot lid rattles a hello.

NARRATOR: Mina takes the route atlas from its special shelf and opens it to the Coast of Small Lights. Three empty circles have been waiting there all night. She presses the stamp down — once for the Lighthouse Kitchen, once for the Tidepool Bridge, once for the Village of Small Lights — and three small gold stations appear on the route line, joined up, finished, yours.

ENDING — HOME BY BEDTIME

NARRATOR: Past the village, the track does what it always does on the first night: it curves, gently, the way a path curves through a garden, and the houses get familiar, and the street lamps get familiar, and there — there is home, with one window still warm.

Bruno slows the train to a stop softer than a sigh. Mina punches the golden ticket one last time — click — a second crescent beside the first, so Alex will always be able to prove the journey happened.

And then Alex is in bed — somehow already in pajamas, somehow already tucked in — and the golden ticket is on the pillow, right where a ticket should be kept, and the atlas is on the shelf with its three gold stations shining faintly in the dark.

MINA (from very far away, or possibly from the bookshelf): “Tomorrow night, traveler, the route continues — the Village of Small Lights, and then… well. One more line, whenever you are ready, and the train will come.”

(End of Night One. The atlas keeps the route; the next night starts exactly where this one stopped.)
