# The Snowline Post Relay

*Plan the route, read the weather, and walk the winter post home — together*

**Category:** Games · **Ages:** Ages 7–11 · **Chinese name:** 雪线邮路接力 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A cooperative route-planning game about carrying the winter post across a small mountain valley before the snowline drops. The board is a map of six villages joined by paths that climb, dip, and cross one river bridge. Each round, a weather card is turned over — clear, wind, or fresh snow — and the team plans one relay leg together: which carrier walks, which path they take, and which letters they hand over at the next village stove. Fresh snow closes the high pass until someone walks the low road with the snowshoes; wind makes the bridge path cost double steps. Nobody wins alone and nobody loses alone: the post either arrives before the third snow bell, or the valley writes a very polite letter of apology and tries again tomorrow. The thinking is real — shortest path is not always safest path — but every decision is made out loud, together, with the map in plain sight.

## Features

- Cooperative from the first move: one shared postbag, one shared plan, no player elimination and no hidden traitor
- A six-village map with three kinds of path — low road, high pass, and river bridge — each with its own weather rule
- Weather cards that change the board, not the players: fresh snow closes the high pass, wind doubles the bridge, clear skies open everything
- Relay legs planned out loud: the team must say the route and the reason before any carrier moves
- Letters with little stories on them — each delivery is read aloud, so finishing a leg always pays out a tiny tale
- The snowshoe token: one carrier can reopen a snowed-in path, but only by walking the long low road first
- Three snow bells as the gentle clock: the game ends in a shared win or a shared, funny, try-again letter — never a blame screen
- A solo mode with the same rules, where one player runs all three carriers

## What's included

- Valley map board (six villages, nine paths, one bridge)
- Weather card deck (clear / wind / fresh snow)
- Three carrier pieces and the snowshoe token
- Letter deck with a one-paragraph story on every envelope
- Route-planning slate: say it, trace it, then walk it
- Snow bell tracker and the valley's apology-letter card

## At a glance

- Fresh snow just closed the high pass; the bridge is open but the wind is rising — the team argues for the low road, and the low road wins
- A letter to the lighthouse keeper's cousin must cross the whole valley in two legs, with a handover at the middle village stove
- The third snow bell is one card away, the postbag holds two letters, and the snowshoes are finally in the right village

## How it works

1. Turn over one weather card and read what it changes on the map
2. The team plans one relay leg out loud: carrier, path, and which letters move
3. Trace the agreed route on the planning slate, then walk the carrier along it
4. Deliver any letters that reached their village and read one aloud
5. If fresh snow fell, ring nothing yet — the snow bell rings only when a snow card is drawn twice in a row, and the third bell ends the game

## Sample content

### Sample Round · Snow on the Pass, Wind on the Bridge (complete worked round)

THE VALLEY. Six villages: Millbrook (start, where the postbag waits), Stove End, Ferry Landing, Highmere, Pinewatch, and Lastlight (where the final letters are due). Paths: Millbrook–Stove End (low road, 2 steps), Stove End–Ferry Landing (low road, 2 steps), Ferry Landing–Lastlight (bridge, 2 steps, doubled in wind), Stove End–Highmere (high pass, 3 steps, closed by fresh snow), Highmere–Pinewatch (low road, 2 steps), Pinewatch–Lastlight (low road, 3 steps), Millbrook–Pinewatch (ridge path, 4 steps), Highmere–Lastlight (high pass, 4 steps, closed by fresh snow), Ferry Landing–Pinewatch (low road, 3 steps). Carriers: Ash starts at Millbrook, Bram at Stove End, Clover at Ferry Landing. The snowshoes start at Pinewatch. Postbag letters: one for Highmere, one for Lastlight, one for Pinewatch.

THE WEATHER. The card turns: FRESH SNOW. Both high-pass paths close until a carrier wearing the snowshoes walks a low road. The snow bell does not ring — it rings only on the second snow card in a row.

THE PLAN, SAID OUT LOUD. The team talks it through. "Highmere's letter can't use the pass. Clover is at Ferry Landing, one low road from Pinewatch, where the snowshoes are. If Clover walks to Pinewatch, delivers the Pinewatch letter, and picks up the snowshoes, next round the pass can reopen. Meanwhile Ash takes the Lastlight letter down the low roads: Millbrook to Stove End this leg." Bram, at Stove End, will be ready to receive it and walk the bridge next round — unless the wind comes up.

THE WALK. Leg one: Ash walks Millbrook–Stove End (2 steps), carrying the Lastlight letter, and hands it to Bram at the stove. Leg two: Clover walks Ferry Landing–Pinewatch (3 steps), delivers the Pinewatch letter, and reads it aloud: "Dear Pinewatch — the mill wheel is fixed, the first flour is ground, and there is a sack with your name on it when the road opens. Come hungry." Clover takes the snowshoes.

END OF ROUND. Delivered: 1 of 3. Snowshoes: with Clover at Pinewatch. Snow bells rung: 0. The team writes one line on the planning slate before the next card: "Bram holds the bridge letter. If wind comes, Ash and Bram swap at Stove End instead."

YOUR TURN (answers in brackets): 1) The next weather card is WIND. Bram is at Stove End with the Lastlight letter. What does the bridge path Ferry Landing–Lastlight cost this round, and which carrier is standing closest to Ferry Landing? [The bridge costs double: 4 steps. Bram at Stove End is one low road (2 steps) from Ferry Landing — Clover is at Pinewatch, 3 steps away.] 2) Snow falls twice in a row later in the game and the third bell rings with the Highmere letter still undelivered. What happens? [The game ends in the shared try-again ending: the valley's apology letter is read aloud, the delivered letters are celebrated, and the same postbag is dealt again tomorrow — nobody is named as the reason.] 3) Why can the snowshoes not reopen the high pass from Pinewatch in one move? [A carrier must walk a low road while carrying them first; Pinewatch's neighbours are reached by low roads, so the pass reopens only after that walk is done.]

RELAY PROMISE: say the route, say the reason, then walk it. A team that plans out loud never loses a letter to the snow.
