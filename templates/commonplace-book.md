# The Commonplace Book

*Keep what you read. Connect what you think. Review what matters.*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Ages 16+ · **Chinese name:** 摘记本 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A commonplace book is the oldest personal knowledge tool there is: one place where you copy out the passages that stopped you, in your own hand, and think next to them. This template gives that practice a gentle structure. Capture a passage in under a minute. Connect it to one earlier entry with a theme thread. Answer one small question Muse asks about it — never a quiz, always an invitation. Then, once a week, a calm 20-minute review resurfaces three old entries beside the new ones, so ideas meet each other instead of piling up. No feeds, no streaks, no scores. Just a book that gets more yours the longer you keep it.

## Features

- Capture flow: passage + source + why it stopped you, in under a minute
- Theme threads: link any entry to an earlier one in one tap
- One gentle question per entry — an invitation, never a quiz
- Weekly review resurfaces 3 older entries beside the new
- No feeds, no streaks, no scores — export your book each season

- Theme threads — watch 'attention', 'craft' and 'enough' grow as entries link together.
- Sunday review — three old entries return to sit beside this week's new ones.

## How it works

1. Capture — copy the passage and add one line on why it stopped you.
2. Connect — pick a theme thread or start one.
3. Reflect — answer Muse's one question in 2–3 sentences.
4. Review — weekly, revisit three older entries and write one synthesis line.

## Sample content

### Entry 1 · On attention (a complete worked entry)

PASSAGE (original, written for this sample): 'Attention is the rarest gift we carry in our pockets. We spend it like small coins all day — a glance here, a worry there — and wonder in the evening why the purse feels light.' — from the sample essay 'Small Coins' included with this template. WHY IT STOPPED ME: I read this on a busy morning and realised I could not name one thing I had looked at properly before noon. THEME THREAD: attention (entry 1 of the thread). MUSE'S QUESTION: 'What is one ordinary thing you pass every day that you have never actually looked at? Give it sixty full seconds tomorrow and write what changed.' MY ANSWER (sample): 'The jacaranda outside the gate. I have walked under it for months and never noticed it drops its blossoms in a spiral, not a scatter. Sixty seconds felt long. That was the point.' WEEKLY REVIEW SYNTHESIS LINE (sample, written Sunday): 'This week attention, craft and enough all turned out to be the same entry wearing three coats: doing one thing as if it mattered.'
