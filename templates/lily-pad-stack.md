# Lily Pad Stack

*Move the whole tower, one leaf at a time*

**Category:** Games · **Ages:** Ages 5–9 · **Chinese name:** 荷叶叠叠乐 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A gentle stacking puzzle on a quiet pond: three lily pads, three leaves in a tower, and one unbreakable rule — a bigger leaf never sits on a smaller one. Players move the whole tower from the home pad to the far pad, one leaf at a time, and discover why the smallest tower takes 3 moves, the next takes 7, and each new leaf doubles the puzzle plus one. Every session is fully worked and checked move by move, so a child can prove the finish is right instead of guessing. No timers, no losing — an illegal stack just wobbles and waits for a better move.

## Features

- One rule, whole puzzle — a bigger leaf never sits on a smaller one
- Fully worked towers: 2 leaves in 3 moves, 3 leaves in 7 moves, checked after every move
- The doubling secret, discovered not told — each new leaf doubles the moves plus one
- Undo-friendly pond: a wobbly stack waits, it never punishes
- Make-your-own challenge: set a start, solve it, then swap with a friend

## At a glance

- Three pads, three leaves, one rule — move the whole tower to the far pad
- Small leaf shuttles, big leaf travels once — the 7-move finish, checked step by step
- Add a fourth leaf and the best count jumps to 15 — double plus one, every time

## How it works

1. Set the tower on the home pad, biggest leaf at the bottom, and read the one rule aloud together
2. Move one leaf at a time to any pad — never place a bigger leaf on a smaller one
3. Count the moves, check the tower landed whole on the far pad, then try to match the best count

## Sample content

### Sample · The Tower That Crossed the Pond (complete worked session)

LILY PAD STACK — a complete first session (about 25 minutes)

YOU NEED: three lily pads in a row — call them Home (left), Middle, and Far (right) — and three leaves: Big, Middle-sized (call it Mid), and Small. Paper circles or real counters all work; the pond is only pretend. One player is the Pond Keeper and reads aloud; the other is the Mover. Swap roles for the second tower. The one rule, read aloud together before anyone touches a leaf: move ONE leaf at a time, and a bigger leaf NEVER sits on a smaller one. If a move would break the rule, the stack wobbles — put the leaf back and choose again. Nothing is lost; the pond waits.

WARM-UP — THE 2-LEAF TOWER (best possible: 3 moves). Tower: Big on the bottom, Small on top, both on Home. Goal: the whole tower on Far. Move 1: Small, Home to Middle. Check: Middle was empty, so the rule holds. Move 2: Big, Home to Far. Check: Far was empty, so the rule holds. Move 3: Small, Middle to Far, landing on Big. Check: Small on Big is allowed — smaller on bigger is always fine. Finished in 3 moves, and 3 is provably best: Big must travel exactly once, and Small must move out of the way before that trip and land back on top after it, so no finish can use fewer than 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 moves.

MAIN EVENT — THE 3-LEAF TOWER (best possible: 7 moves). Tower on Home, bottom to top: Big, Mid, Small. Goal: the whole tower on Far, same order. Here is a complete finish — read each move, make it, then say the check out loud before the next move. Move 1: Small, Home to Far. Check: Far empty — holds. Move 2: Mid, Home to Middle. Check: Middle empty — holds. Move 3: Small, Far to Middle, onto Mid. Check: Small on Mid — allowed. Move 4: Big, Home to Far. Check: Far is empty again because Small left it — holds, and this is the only move Big ever makes. Move 5: Small, Middle to Home. Check: Home empty — holds. Move 6: Mid, Middle to Far, onto Big. Check: Mid on Big — allowed. Move 7: Small, Home to Far, onto Mid. Check: Small on Mid — allowed. Finished: Far now holds Big, Mid, Small in order — the whole tower crossed in 7 moves.

WHY 7 IS BEST (say it simply): Big can only move when both smaller leaves sit together on the one spare pad, and getting them there takes 3 moves — exactly the warm-up pattern. Big moves once. Then the two small leaves must re-stack on top of Big, which takes the same 3 moves again. So the best finish is 3 + 1 + 3 = 7 moves, every time. No shortcut exists, and any finish in 7 has truly solved it.

THE DOUBLING SECRET. Write the best counts in a row: 1 leaf takes 1 move, 2 leaves take 3 moves, 3 leaves take 7 moves. Each count is double the one before, plus one: 1, then 2×1+1=3, then 2×3+1=7. So a 4-leaf tower takes 2×7+1=15 moves — that is the Challenge tower, and the same shuttle pattern solves it: stack the top three on one spare pad (7 moves), move Big once (1 move), re-stack the three on top (7 moves), 7+1+7=15. A 5-leaf tower would take 31. Players who spot this have found the real treasure of the pond: a big puzzle is just a smaller puzzle, twice, with one big move in the middle.

MAKE YOUR OWN (last 5 minutes): the Mover sets any legal starting stack (bigger never on smaller, anywhere on the three pads), writes down where the tower must finish, and the Keeper must solve it and count the moves. Then swap. Keeper's fairness check before play: a start is fair only if every stack already obeys the one rule — if a bigger leaf sits on a smaller one anywhere, fix the start first.

POND KEEPER'S CLOSING LINE: 'You did not guess your way across — you proved it, move by move. The pond remembers: small puzzle, twice, one big move in the middle.'
