# Paragraph Pioneers

*Build clear paragraphs — topic sentence, details, and a strong finish*

**Category:** Education · **Ages:** Ages 7–11 · **Chinese name:** 段落先锋 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A paragraph-writing studio for young writers who can already build a sentence and are ready to organize ideas. Each session teaches one clear structure — a topic sentence that makes a promise, supporting details in a sensible order, and a closing sentence that finishes the thought — then guides the writer from outline to draft to a friendly checklist revision. Writers choose a goal (tell in order, explain, describe, persuade gently, or compare), get sentence starters and a word bank matched to their support level, and finish with a paragraph they can read aloud with pride. Parent view shows the outline, the draft, and which checklist steps were used — never a grade or ranking.

## Features

- One structure per session: topic sentence → details in order → closing sentence
- Outline-first planner with sentence starters and a themed word bank
- Five writing goals: narrate, explain, describe, persuade gently, compare
- Friendly checklist revision — no grades, no rankings, no red marks
- Parent view: outline, draft, and checklist steps used

## At a glance

- Start with a promise — the topic sentence tells the reader exactly what is coming
- Details in order: first, next, then, finally — or spatial and how-it-works orders
- Finish strong: a closing sentence that restates the promise in fresh words

## How it works

1. Pick a writing goal and topic theme, then open the planner: one promise line, three detail lines, one closing line
2. Read the worked example and find its three parts — topic sentence, details in order, closing sentence in fresh words
3. Draft your own paragraph from the outline, then revise with the friendly checklist and read it aloud

## Sample content

### Sample Session · The Community Garden Paragraph (complete worked session)

SESSION GOAL: Write one paragraph that tells events in order. Structure: 1 topic sentence (the promise) + 3 detail sentences in time order + 1 closing sentence.

WARM-UP (say it before you write it): Finish this topic sentence out loud: 'Saturday morning at the community garden was ___ because ___ .' A good topic sentence makes a promise the rest of the paragraph must keep. Example promise: 'Saturday morning at the community garden was busy because everyone had a job to do.'

LEARN THE THREE PARTS:
1) TOPIC SENTENCE — one sentence that names the topic and the main idea. Test: a reader who reads ONLY this sentence should know what the paragraph is about.
2) DETAIL SENTENCES — 3 sentences that prove or explain the promise, in an order the reader can follow. For events, use time order and signal words: First, Next, Then, Finally.
3) CLOSING SENTENCE — restates the promise in fresh words. It is NOT a copy of sentence 1. Compare: Copy: 'Saturday morning was busy because everyone had a job.' Fresh: 'By lunchtime, the busy morning had turned rows of bare soil into a garden ready to grow.'

WORKED EXAMPLE — read it, then find the parts:
[Topic] Saturday morning at the community garden was busy because everyone had a job to do. [Detail 1] First, Milo and Ana carried the watering cans to the bean rows and soaked the soil until it turned dark and soft. [Detail 2] Next, their neighbor Mr. Park showed them how to press a sunflower seed into each hole with one finger, no deeper than a thumbnail. [Detail 3] Then everyone worked together to stretch the string line straight, so the new rows would grow in neat lines instead of crooked ones. [Closing] By lunchtime, the busy morning had turned rows of bare soil into a garden ready to grow.
Check the promise: busy + everyone had a job — Detail 1 (carrying/watering), Detail 2 (learning/planting), Detail 3 (working together) all keep it. Order check: First → Next → Then → By lunchtime. The order cannot be shuffled without confusing the reader, and that is how you know the order is right.

WORD BANK for this session: community, row, soak, press, stretch, neat, crooked, ready. Sentence starters if you want them: 'First, we ___ .' / 'Next, ___ showed us how to ___ .' / 'My favorite job was ___ because ___ .'

GUIDED PRACTICE (do this before your own paragraph): (a) Underline the topic sentence in the worked example and circle the word that states the main idea (busy). (b) Number the detail sentences 1-2-3 and write the signal word above each (First, Next, Then). (c) Cover the closing sentence and say your own version out loud — it must use at least two NEW words that were not in sentence 1.

NOW WRITE YOURS: Pick one real or imagined morning: a kitchen, a market, a workshop, a rainy-day library — anywhere people have jobs to do. Fill the planner: Promise (topic sentence): ___ . Detail 1 (First): ___ . Detail 2 (Next): ___ . Detail 3 (Then): ___ . Closing (fresh words): ___ . Then copy the five sentences together as one paragraph, indent the first line, and read it aloud once.

FRIENDLY CHECKLIST (tick each one you did — there are no grades here): □ My topic sentence makes a clear promise. □ My three details are in an order a reader can follow, with signal words. □ Every detail helps keep my promise (no stray sentences). □ My closing sentence uses fresh words, not a copy. □ I read my paragraph aloud and fixed any sentence that sounded tangled.

PARENT NOTE: Ask the writer to point to the promise and to one detail they are proudest of. Praise the order and the fresh closing words specifically. If a detail wanders off-promise, ask 'Does this sentence help keep your promise?' and let the writer decide to move it or cut it — revision choices belong to the writer.
