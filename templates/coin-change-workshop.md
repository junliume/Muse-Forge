# The Coin & Change Workshop

*Count coins, make change, and check it back — money maths you can hold*

**Category:** Education · **Ages:** Ages 6–9 · **Chinese name:** 硬币找零工坊 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A hands-on money workshop where learners recognise coins, count mixed handfuls, pay exact amounts, and make change by counting up — then check every answer backwards so the maths proves itself. Every lesson starts at a market stall, ticket window or snack counter, teaches one clear money skill with visible coins, then practises it in the same order a real purchase happens: see the price, choose the coins, count what you handed over, work out the change, and count the change back to be sure. Difficulty and coin sets adapt to the age band, hints show the coins rather than just the numbers, and nothing is timed.

## Features

- Coin recognition first: name it, sort it, say its value before any adding
- Count-up change method — from the price up to the coin handed over, step by step
- Check-it-back step on every purchase: price + change must equal what you paid
- Exact-pay challenges: choose coins that make the price with nothing left over
- Real counters to practise at: market stall, ticket window, snack counter, book fair table
- Mixed-handful counting sorted biggest-first, the way real tills are counted
- No timers, no speed scores — careful counting grows calmly

## At a glance

- A handful on the tray: one quarter, two dimes and a nickel — sorted biggest-first, that is 25 + 10 + 10 + 5 = 50 cents
- The count-up track: the ticket costs 65 cents and you hand over a dollar — count 65, 70, 75, 100: the change is a nickel, a quarter, and 35 cents in all
- Check it back: 65 + 35 = 100, exactly what you handed over — the change is right

## How it works

1. Meet the coins on the tray and sort a mixed handful biggest-first
2. Learn one money skill — counting a handful, paying exactly, or counting up change
3. Run a real counter purchase in order: price, coins, payment, change
4. Check it back: price + change must equal the amount handed over

## Sample content

### Sample Lesson · Change at the Ticket Window: Counting Mixed Coins and Making Change (complete worked lesson)

WELCOME TO THE WORKSHOP. At the Coin & Change Workshop, money is never just numbers on a page — it sits on a tray where you can see it, sort it and count it. Today we learn the workshop's two big skills and use them at a real ticket window.

THE COINS — KNOW THEM FIRST. A penny is worth 1 cent. A nickel is worth 5 cents. A dime is worth 10 cents — smaller than a nickel, but worth more, which is why we always look at the coin, not just its size. A quarter is worth 25 cents. A dollar bill is worth 100 cents.

COUNT A HANDFUL, BIGGEST-FIRST. The tray holds one quarter, two dimes, one nickel and three pennies. Sort biggest-first and count in a chain: quarter 25, dime 35, dime 45, nickel 50, penny 51, penny 52, penny 53. The handful is worth 53 cents. Counting biggest-first means the numbers only ever get bigger by the coin you just added — no starting over.

PAY EXACTLY. The window sells a ride ticket for 40 cents. Pay exactly, with no change back: one quarter (25), one dime (35), one nickel (40). Three coins, exactly 40 cents. A second way also works: four dimes. Exact pay is a puzzle — more than one answer can be right.

MAKE CHANGE BY COUNTING UP. Now the bigger ticket costs 65 cents and you hand over one dollar bill — 100 cents. Do not subtract yet. Count UP from the price to what you handed over, using coins as stepping stones: start at 65. Add a nickel: 70. Add a nickel: 75. Add a quarter: 100. You have arrived. The coins you stepped with ARE the change: nickel + nickel + quarter = 5 + 5 + 25 = 35 cents.

CHECK IT BACK. Every purchase at the workshop ends with the check: price + change must equal what you handed over. 65 + 35 = 100. It matches the dollar bill, so the change is right. If it had not matched, the counting-up would be done again, slowly — the check catches mistakes before the customer walks away.

A SECOND PURCHASE. A snack costs 27 cents. You hand over two quarters — 50 cents. Count up from 27: add three pennies — 28, 29, 30. Add a dime: 40. Add a dime: 50. Change: three pennies and two dimes = 3 + 10 + 10 = 23 cents. Check it back: 27 + 23 = 50. Correct.

YOUR TURN (answers in brackets): 1) Count this handful: one dime, one nickel, four pennies. [Sort biggest-first: 10, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19 — 19 cents.] 2) Pay exactly 30 cents using a quarter. [Quarter 25, then a nickel: 25 + 5 = 30 cents.] 3) A pencil costs 45 cents and you hand over 50 cents. What is the change? [Count up: 45, 50 — one nickel, 5 cents. Check: 45 + 5 = 50.] 4) A sticker costs 58 cents and you hand over a dollar. Count up and give the change. [Start at 58: add two pennies to reach 60, then four dimes — 70, 80, 90, 100. Change: 2 + 40 = 42 cents. Check: 58 + 42 = 100.] 5) Is this change right? Price 74 cents, paid with a dollar, change given 30 cents. [No. Check it back: 74 + 30 = 104, not 100. Count up from 74: a penny to 75, a quarter to 100 — change should be 26 cents. 74 + 26 = 100.] 6) Make 99 cents with the fewest coins you can. [Three quarters (75), two dimes (95), four pennies (99) — nine coins.]

WORKSHOP PROMISE: sort it, count it, pay it, count the change up — then check it back. A learner who checks is never short-changed by a silly mistake.
