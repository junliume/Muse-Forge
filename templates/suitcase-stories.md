# Suitcase Stories

*Open the suitcase, pick a souvenir, hear a tale from a far-away place.*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 6–10 · **Chinese name:** 行李箱故事会 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A traveling storyteller arrives with a suitcase of souvenirs. Each session the child picks one souvenir and hears one complete tale from the place it came from, steering it with 3 gentle choices — or free voice wishes, or listen-only. Every tale ends in the same session: no cliffhangers at bedtime. Finished tales pin a stamp on the keepsake Story Map, and a Parent Listening Log keeps every tale title, the choices made, and listening time — silent and parent-only.

## Features

- One complete tale per session — no bedtime cliffhangers
- Pick a souvenir, hear its tale: bell, shell, ticket, pinecone, key…
- 3 gentle choice points steer the story — or add a free voice wish
- Keepsake Story Map grows one stamp per finished tale
- Parent Listening Log: titles, choices, and listening time

## Sample content

### Tale 1 · The Bell of Brine Bay (complete tale, with the 3 choice points as they appear)

The souvenir Sam picks is a small brass bell, green at the edges from sea air.

The tale: In the seaside town of Brine Bay, young ferry pilot Pell has one job each foggy morning — climb the harbor tower and ring the brass bell so the fishing boats can hear their way home. One grey morning Pell finds the bell rope frayed to its last threads.

CHOICE 1 (as shown in-app): “What should Pell do first? A) Ring very gently and hope. B) Climb down and fetch the spare rope from the lighthouse store room. C) Ask Old Marta, who mends nets, for help.” In this telling Sam chose B.

Pell fetches the spare rope — but the store room key hangs on a hook shaped like a fish, and the hook is empty.

CHOICE 2: “Where is the fish key? A) Marta borrowed it to open the bait shed. B) It fell into the bucket of silver fish. C) The harbor cat is sitting on it.” Sam chose C — the harbor cat, Biscuit, is indeed sitting on the key, warm and pleased, and trades it for a scratch behind the ears.

Rope replaced, Pell climbs back up — and the fog thickens; from the tower Pell can hear three boats, but also a fourth, fainter sound: a small rowboat with a lost young rower who cannot hear the bell over his own splashing.

CHOICE 3: “How can Pell reach the rowboat? A) Ring the bell in the pattern Marta taught: two short, one long — 'this way home.' B) Shout as loud as possible. C) Wait for the fog to lift.” Sam chose A.

Two short rings, one long — the pattern every Brine Bay child learns — and the little rowboat turns toward the sound, following bell by bell until its bow bumps the pier, safe. That evening Marta re-braids the old rope into a collar bell for Biscuit, 'harbor master of keys', and Pell writes the day in the tower log: 'Fog: thick. Rope: new. Boats home: all of them.' The tale ends; a brass-bell stamp appears on Sam's Story Map.
