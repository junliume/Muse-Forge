# The Domino Design Lab

*Build a chain, predict the fall, then watch it run*

**Category:** Games · **Ages:** Ages 6–11 · **Chinese name:** 多米诺设计实验室 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A hands-on design game where the player plans a domino chain on paper or a grid first, predicts counts, colors, turns and total run time, builds it, then topples it and checks every prediction. No speed rewards, no timers pressuring the child — prediction and careful building are the game. Failures are design data: a chain that stops teaches exactly where the spacing or turn failed, and the lab card helps fix that one spot and re-run.

## Features

- Plan → Predict → Build → Topple → Check loop on every level
- Counting, repeating patterns, turns, bridges and ramps as real design problems
- Spacing rule and timing estimate the player can verify with a ruler and a clock
- Fix-one-spot repair cards — no fail state, every stop has a reason and a fix

## What's included

## Sample content

### Sample · Level 1: The Bakery Delivery Run (complete level with checked answers)

LEVEL CARD — Player: the designer · Ages 6–11 · About 15 min · Theme: Bakery delivery · The loop on every level: Plan → Predict → Build → Topple → Check.

THE STORY
Sam is the baker's helper. The bread cart is loaded and ready at the front door, but the baker is at the back counter with floury hands and cannot see the door. Sam's job: build a domino chain that carries a signal from the front door all the way to the small brass bell on the counter. When the last domino taps the bell lever, the bell rings — and the baker knows the bread cart is ready to go.

SETUP — THE DOMINO BANK AND THE SPACING RULE
Your domino bank holds 48 dominoes. Every domino in this lab is 2 units tall. The lab spacing rule is simple, and you can check it with a ruler: space the dominoes 1 unit apart — exactly half the height — so each falling domino can always reach the next one. Space them wider and the fall falls short; space them closer and the chain bunches up. One unit apart is the design that works.

STEP 1 — PLAN THE ROUTE (draw it first)
On the plan sheet, draw the route before you touch a single domino. The route has three segments:
Segment 1 — Front Door Run: 14 dominoes in a straight line from the front door.
Segment 2 — Counter Turn & Ramp: 9 dominoes — 3 for the curved turn plus 6 climbing the low ramp.
Segment 3 — Bell Run: 11 dominoes in a straight line to the bell. The last domino falls against a small lever, and the lever rings the bell.
Now count the plan: 14 + 9 + 11 = 34 dominoes used. The bank holds 48, so the spare count is 48 − 34 = 14 dominoes left in the bank. Write both numbers on the plan sheet: Used 34, Spare 14.

STEP 2 — PREDICT THE COLORS
The chain is built in a repeating color pattern: Red, Blue, Blue, Yellow — a 4-domino pattern that repeats along the whole run. So domino #1 is Red, #2 is Blue, #3 is Blue, #4 is Yellow, #5 is Red again, and so on.
Checked question: What color is domino #22? Work it like a designer, not a guesser. Divide: 22 ÷ 4 = 5 full patterns, which covers 20 dominoes, with 2 left over. So #22 is the 2nd domino of the next pattern — and the 2nd domino in Red, Blue, Blue, Yellow is Blue. Answer: #22 is Blue. ✓
Two more checks on the same pattern: #20 is Yellow, because 20 is exactly 5 full patterns, so #20 is the last domino of the pattern. And #21 is Red, because a new pattern starts there.

STEP 3 — PREDICT THE TIME
In this spacing, each domino knocks the next one about 0.3 seconds later. How many gaps are there between 34 dominoes? One less than the dominoes: 33 gaps. So the predicted run time is 33 × 0.3 = 9.9 seconds — about 10 seconds — plus the lever tap and the bell ring at the very end. Write the prediction on the sheet before anyone pushes anything: Predicted: about 10 seconds. When you topple the chain, run a clock alongside it and write the real time next to the prediction. Close counts as a design win. Far off is not a failure — it is a clue to check the spacing.

STEP 4 — BUILD IT
Build segment by segment, exactly as drawn: 14 straight, then the turn of 3 and the ramp climb of 6, then 11 straight to the bell. Use the ruler to keep the 1-unit spacing, and keep the color pattern going — Red, Blue, Blue, Yellow — without breaking the repeat at the joins between segments.

STEP 5 — THE GAP PROBLEM (worked, with the fix)
At the top of the ramp there is a 3-unit gap with no shelf. Stop and think before you topple: a standing domino is only 2 units tall, so it cannot reach across a 3-unit gap. The chain would stop right there — 3 is greater than 2, so the fall cannot reach.
Fix-one-spot card — slide the ramp: slide the ramp 1.5 units closer to the run. Now do the gap math again: 3 − 1.5 = 1.5 units remaining. Is 1.5 less than the 2-unit domino height? Yes — 1.5 < 2, so the falling domino can reach across, and the chain continues. One spot fixed, nothing else rebuilt.
One more designer touch: stand 1 spare domino flat behind the bell lever as a safety stop, so the lever domino does not slide away when it falls. That uses 1 spare, so the spare count after the fix is 14 − 1 = 13.

STEP 6 — TOPPLE AND CHECK
Push the first domino gently and watch the whole design run: down the Front Door Run, around the Counter Turn, up the ramp, across the fixed gap, and down the Bell Run to the lever. Ding! Fill in the Check Box:
Used 34 ✓ · Spare after fix 13 ✓ · #22 Blue ✓ · Predicted about 10 seconds · Real time: ______ (write yours here) · One thing I would change: ______________________

THE DELIVERY
When the bell rings, the baker looks up, wipes the flour on an apron, and smiles. Sam wheels the pretend bread cart out through the front door, past the very first domino of the run, and the whole bakery smells — in pretend, at least — of warm bread. The signal worked because the design worked: planned, predicted, built, toppled, checked.

PARENT / DESIGN NOTE: There is no collecting and no scoring for speed in this lab, and no real baking — the bread cart is pretend. An adult helps with the ramp and any books used as supports. If a run stops, treat it as design data: find the one spot, fix that one spot with a repair card, and re-run.
