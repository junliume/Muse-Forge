# Table Talk Studio

*Great questions, easy hosting — a conversation night that flows*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Grown-ups · **Chinese name:** 餐桌谈话工作室 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A hosting studio for a conversation night that actually flows. Pick a gathering type, a group size and a question depth, and the app deals a sequenced deck — a gentle opener, two themed rounds, and a closing question everyone answers. Built-in listening rules keep it kind: one story at a time, ask one follow-up before you share your own, and anyone may pass with no explanation. No scoring, no debate, no mystery to solve — just better questions than 'so, what do you do?'

## Features

- Sequenced decks: opener, two themed rounds, closing question
- Three depths: Light & playful, Warm & personal, Deep & reflective
- Kind listening rules and an always-allowed pass
- Host flow with timings so the night never stalls

## Sample content

### Sample Evening · Evening One: Beginnings (complete hosted session)

EVENING ONE: BEGINNINGS — a complete hosted session. Setup: Dinner at home · 3–4 people · About 60 min · Warm & personal · Themes: Beginnings & firsts + Food & home · Round-robin cards. Host: Alex. This evening deals exactly 8 cards: 1 opener + 3 in Round 1 + 3 in Round 2 + 1 closing.

(1) HOST OPENING (about 5 min). Alex says: “Welcome, everyone — thanks for coming. Tonight isn't a quiz and there's nothing to win. We have eight cards and about an hour, and my only job is to keep us moving gently. Here are our two listening rules. Rule one: one story at a time — when someone is telling theirs, we listen all the way to the end. Rule two: ask one follow-up question before you share your own story — it shows the teller we were really listening. And one more thing: anyone may pass on any card, at any time, with no explanation and no teasing. A pass is always a complete answer. Ready? First card.”

(2) OPENER CARD (1 card — everyone answers in turn). CARD: “What was the first meal you ever cooked entirely by yourself — and how did it turn out?” Alex answers first, briefly, to set the tone; then each Guest answers in turn. (Running total: 1 card.)

(3) ROUND 1 — BEGINNINGS & FIRSTS (3 cards, round-robin). CARD 1: “Tell us about a first day — a first day of school, of work, or in a new place. What small detail from it do you still remember clearly?” Host note — a good follow-up sounds like: “What were you most hoping for that morning?” CARD 2: “What is something you were a complete beginner at not long ago? What surprised you most about learning it?” Host note — a good follow-up sounds like: “Who helped you most at the very start?” CARD 3: “Describe the first home you remember living in. Which room comes back to you first?” Host note — a good follow-up sounds like: “What sound or smell belongs to that room?” (Running total: 4 cards.)

(4) ROUND 2 — FOOD & HOME (3 cards, round-robin). CARD 1: “Which dish tastes like home to you, no matter who makes it? Who made it best?” Host note — a good follow-up sounds like: “When did you last have it, really well made?” CARD 2: “Tell us about a meal that went wrong — a flop, a burn, a mix-up. What happened, and could you laugh about it?” Host note — a good follow-up sounds like: “Did you ever dare to make it again?” CARD 3: “If your kitchen could keep only three tools, which three would you save — and what would you miss most?” Host note — a good follow-up sounds like: “What is the oldest thing in your kitchen?” (Running total: 7 cards.)

(5) CLOSING CARD (1 card — everyone answers) + CLOSE. CARD: “What is one beginning — big or small — you would like to make in the next year?” Everyone answers in turn; passes are still fine. Alex's closing words: “Thank you — for the stories, and for listening the way you did. That is the whole evening: eight cards, and I know you all a little better than I did at dinner.” Optional follow-up prompt for the next gathering: “Before next time, notice one small beginning happening around you — a first attempt, a new habit, a fresh start — and bring it with you. We'll open with those.” (Final count: 8 cards — 1 opener + 3 + 3 + 1 closing.)
