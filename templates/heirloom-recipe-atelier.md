# The Heirloom Recipe Atelier

*Turn a beloved dish into a keepsake recipe story*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Adults · **Chinese name:** 家传食谱工坊 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A gentle atelier for grown-ups who want to keep a dish — and the story that travels with it. Pick a recipe you love, answer a few warm prompts about who taught it to you and when it appears at the table, and the atelier shapes it into a keepsake: a clear, cookable recipe card plus a short story page that holds the memory without inventing any facts for you. You stay in charge of every detail — measures are checked for cookability, vague handfuls are translated into honest ranges with the original words kept beside them, and anything you have not said is left as a blank to fill, never filled in for you. End each session with a printable card, a shopping list, and one small variation to try next time.

## Features

- A cookable recipe card plus a story page — both from your own words
- Handfuls and pinches translated to honest ranges, original wording kept
- Nothing invented: blanks stay blank until you fill them
- Shopping list and one gentle variation with every keepsake

## Sample content

### Sample · Keepsake One: Mara's Tomato and White Bean Stew (complete recipe story)

STORY PAGE — Mara learned this stew on a rainy Tuesday from her neighbour Tomas, who always made it when the building's boiler broke and neighbours gathered in the one warm kitchen. His rule was simple: 'Beans first, tomatoes second, and never rush the onions.' Mara has made it every winter since, always in the same blue pot, always with an extra bowl set out 'just in case someone knocks.'

THE RECIPE CARD — Tomato and White Bean Stew. Serves 4. Prep 15 minutes. Cook 35 minutes. Total 50 minutes.

INGREDIENTS: 2 tablespoons olive oil; 1 large yellow onion, finely chopped (about 200 g); 3 garlic cloves, minced; 1 teaspoon smoked paprika; 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin; 400 g canned chopped tomatoes (one standard can); 2 x 400 g cans white beans (cannellini or butter beans), drained and rinsed (about 480 g drained beans total); 500 ml vegetable stock; 1 bay leaf; 3/4 teaspoon fine salt, plus more to taste; 1/2 teaspoon black pepper; 100 g baby spinach (about 3 large handfuls — Tomas's 'three handfuls', kept here beside the weight); juice of 1/2 lemon; chopped parsley to serve (optional).

METHOD: Step 1 — Soften: heat the olive oil in a heavy pot over medium heat. Add the onion and a pinch of the salt and cook 7–8 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and golden at the edges. Do not rush this step — it is the stew's whole foundation. Step 2 — Bloom: add the garlic, smoked paprika and cumin and stir for 30 seconds until fragrant. Step 3 — Simmer: add the tomatoes, beans, stock and bay leaf. Bring to a gentle boil, then lower to a steady simmer, partially covered, for 22 minutes, stirring twice. The stew should thicken enough that a spoon leaves a brief trail. Step 4 — Finish: remove the bay leaf. Stir in the spinach a handful at a time until just wilted, about 1 minute. Add the lemon juice, salt and pepper, then taste and adjust: a little more lemon if it tastes flat, a little more salt if it tastes thin. Step 5 — Serve: rest 5 minutes off the heat. Serve in warm bowls with crusty bread. YIELD CHECK: about 1.6 litres total — four generous bowls of about 400 ml each.

KEEPER'S NOTES (kept in Mara's words): 'Tomas used dried beans soaked overnight. Canned beans are my weekday version and he pretended not to notice.' Freezer note: keeps 3 months without the spinach — add fresh spinach when reheating. Variation for next time: swap smoked paprika for 1 teaspoon of mild curry powder and finish with coconut milk instead of lemon for a softer, rounder bowl.

SHOPPING LIST (for one batch, serves 4): olive oil (pantry), 1 large yellow onion, 1 head garlic, smoked paprika, ground cumin, 1 x 400 g can chopped tomatoes, 2 x 400 g cans white beans, vegetable stock, bay leaves, baby spinach 100 g, 1 lemon, parsley (optional), crusty bread to serve.
