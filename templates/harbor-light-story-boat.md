# The Harbor Light Story Boat

*A floating library that sails one gentle story at a time*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 4–9 · **Chinese name:** 港湾灯塔故事船 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A gentle co-created storytelling voyage aboard a little library boat that visits a different quiet harbor each session. The child chooses the next harbor light color, who is waiting on the dock, and what book the boat should carry — Muse tells one slow chapter at a time, repeats the child's ideas back to confirm, and never rushes ahead. Each voyage ends with a one-sentence harbor log the child dictates. Designed for bedtime and calm daytime reading: short sentences for younger children, richer harbor detail for older ones, no scores, no timers, no ranking.

## Sample content

### Sample · Voyage One: The Morning the Bell Rang Twice (complete opening voyage)

THE HARBOR LIGHT STORY BOAT — VOYAGE ONE: THE MORNING THE BELL RANG TWICE

The library boat was called Beacon, and it was not a fast boat. It carried books instead of fish, and its bell rang once when it arrived and once when a story was ready — never twice in a row. Until this morning.

Alex stood on the dock in soft morning light. The water went lap, lap against the posts. Beacon came around the harbor wall with its shelves visible through the windows, and its captain, a kind gull named Gulliver the Narrator (no relation to any other Gulliver — this one only loved stories), tipped his small blue cap.

'One ring for arrival,' Gulliver said. Ding.
He paused. 'And one ring because today's book chose itself.' Ding.

On the reading table lay a book with a lighthouse on the cover and no title on the spine. The crab librarian, Cleo, clicked her claws very quietly, the way librarians do when they are excited and trying not to show it. 'It was shelved under L,' she whispered, 'for Light, and for Listening.'

Alex chose the first things, as every voyager does:
- The harbor light today would be warm gold.
- The friend waiting at the next dock would be the turtle keeper, Old Tam, who moves slowly and remembers every story he has ever heard.
- The book Beacon would carry was the lighthouse book with no title.

Gulliver wrote all three choices in the harbor log, exactly as Alex said them, and read them back: 'Warm gold light. Old Tam waiting. The lighthouse book.' Alex nodded. Only then did the story begin.

Chapter: The Light That Listened
The lighthouse at Harbor One did not just shine — it listened. When boats told it where they had been, its light grew a little warmer. When no one had visited for a long time, its light grew quiet and pale, not sad, just waiting.

'What should we tell it first?' Gulliver asked, and waited. He is very good at waiting.

Alex said the boat should tell the lighthouse about the morning the bell rang twice, because that had never happened before, and lighthouses like first-time things.

So Beacon told it, in one slow sentence at a time, and the light turned warm gold, just as Alex had chosen. Old Tam, arriving slowly on the next dock, heard the last sentence and smiled. 'That,' he said, 'is a story worth shelving under L.'

The voyage close (read slowly): The bell rings once. The book is closed gently. Alex dictates the harbor log — one sentence, in Alex's own words — and Beacon sails only as far as the harbor wall, ready for tomorrow.

Harbor Log (example, dictated): 'Today the bell rang twice because a book chose us, and we told the lighthouse so its light would stay gold.'

Parent note: No scores, no speed, no next-chapter pressure. The next voyage starts only when the child asks.
