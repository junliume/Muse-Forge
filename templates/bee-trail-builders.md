# Bee Trail Builders

*A cooperative trail-planning board game from hive to flowers*

**Category:** Games · **Ages:** Ages 6–10 · **Chinese name:** 蜜蜂小径建造师 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A cooperative board-game template you can play on paper or on screen: players plan a bee's trail from the hive to the flower patch, visiting every sunflower on the way while stepping around the pond and the stone. There is no racing and no losing — the group wins together when the bee gets home with a full honey trail, and every board can be replayed with a bridge tile or a new obstacle to change the puzzle. It builds route planning, counting, spatial turns (left, right, forward) and kind cooperation, with a helper that hints instead of answering.

## Features

- Cooperative trail planning — the group wins together, no racing or elimination
- Every board is a real puzzle — visit all sunflowers, avoid pond and stone, no repeated squares
- Bridge tiles and new obstacles replay the same board as a fresh puzzle
- Helper hints first — the answer path is only shown after the group has tried

## What's included

- Trail board generator
- Worked Level One with verified solution
- Bridge-tile replay rounds
- Parent trail log

## At a glance

- A 4 by 4 garden board — hive at A1, flower patch at D4, pond and stone blocking the middle
- Plan together, then move — one orthogonal step at a time, visiting B1, D2 and C3 sunflowers
- Lay the bridge tile over the pond and the same board becomes a brand-new, shorter puzzle

## Sample content

### Sample Level · Level One: The First Bee Trail (complete playable session, with verified solution)

LEVEL ONE: THE FIRST BEE TRAIL — a complete playable session (about 25 minutes)

YOU NEED: this board described below (draw it on paper as a 4 by 4 grid, or play it on screen), one bee counter (a button or coin works), three sunflower counters, and one bridge tile (a small strip of paper). Columns are lettered A, B, C, D from west (left) to east (right). Rows are numbered 1, 2, 3, 4 from north (top) to south (bottom). So A1 is the top-left square and D4 is the bottom-right square.

SET THE BOARD: Hive at A1 (the bee starts here). Flower Patch at D4 (the bee must finish here). Pond at B3 — blocked, the bee cannot step on water. Stone at C2 — blocked, the bee cannot step on the stone. Sunflowers on B1, D2 and C3 — the bee must visit all three, in any order, before finishing.

THE RULES (read aloud together): 1) On each move the bee steps exactly one square north, south, east or west — never diagonally. 2) The bee may not step on the Pond (B3) or the Stone (C2). 3) The bee may not step on the same square twice in one trail. 4) The bee must visit all three sunflowers (B1, D2, C3) and then finish on the Flower Patch (D4). 5) Before moving, the group says the whole planned trail out loud, square by square. Only then does the bee move. If the plan breaks a rule, the helper gives a hint — never the answer — and the group plans again.

WARM-UP (2 minutes): Point and say: Which squares touch the Hive at A1? (Answer: B1 to the east and A2 to the south — A1 is a corner, so it has exactly two neighbours.) Which sunflower is closest to the Hive? (Answer: B1, one step east.) Which blocked square sits in the middle of the board? (Answer: the Stone at C2 and the Pond at B3 both guard the middle, which is why the trail must bend.)

PLAY ROUND 1 — PLAN THE TRAIL (about 10 minutes): Ask the group: can the bee go straight along the top row and down the right side? Trace it: A1, B1, C1, D1, D2, D3, D4. That trail visits B1 and D2, but it never visits the C3 sunflower, so it does not finish the level. Ask: where can the bee turn south to catch C3? The only open way down to C3 on the right side is through D3, stepping west into C3 — but then the bee must leave C3 without repeating a square, so it steps south to C4 and east into D4. Now say the full planned trail aloud together, counting the moves on your fingers.

THE VERIFIED SOLUTION (check the group's trail against this, square by square): A1 (Hive, start) → B1 (Sunflower 1) → C1 → D1 → D2 (Sunflower 2) → D3 → C3 (Sunflower 3) → C4 → D4 (Flower Patch, finish). That is 8 moves and 9 squares. Verify it together: every step moves exactly one square orthogonally (east, east, east, south, south, west, south, east); no step lands on the Pond B3 or the Stone C2; no square repeats; all three sunflowers B1, D2 and C3 are visited; and the trail ends on D4. A group that finds a different trail obeying all five rules has also solved the level — compare the two trails and count which one is shorter, and say why.

SCORE THE HONEY (counting practice, about 3 minutes): The bee earns 1 honey drop for each sunflower visited (3 drops) and 2 bonus drops for finishing with no rule broken on the first full plan. A perfect Round 1 scores 5 drops. Write the score in the trail log — the score is the group's, never one player's, and it is never compared with anyone else's.

ROUND 2 — THE BRIDGE TILE (about 7 minutes): Lay the bridge tile over the Pond at B3. The bridge square may now be stepped on, exactly once. Reset the bee to A1 and plan again. Worked example to check against: A1 → A2 → B2 → B3 (across the bridge) → C3 (Sunflower) → C4 → D4 (Flower Patch). That is 6 moves — shorter than Round 1 — but it visits only one sunflower, so it does NOT finish the level by itself. The group's challenge: find a bridge trail that still visits B1 and D2 as well. Hint if needed: visit B1 first, come back west is not allowed (no repeats) — so think about D2 last, after the bridge. One verified bridge trail: A1 → B1 (Sunflower 1) → B2 → B3 (bridge) → C3 (Sunflower 2) → C4 → D4 is only 6 moves but misses D2; keep planning — a full verified trail is A1 → B1 → C1 → D1 → D2 (Sunflower 2) → D3 → C3 (Sunflower 3) → C4 → D4, which is the Round 1 trail and does not need the bridge at all. Honest lesson, said warmly: sometimes the new tool does not shorten the real job, and noticing that is the smartest move of the day. Score Round 2 for sunflowers and planning, not for speed.

ROUND 3 — MAKE YOUR OWN BOARD (about 5 minutes): The group moves ONE sunflower and ONE blocked square to new squares, keeping the Hive at A1 and the Flower Patch at D4. Before playing, the group must prove the new board can be solved: say a full legal trail aloud, square by square, checking every rule. If no legal trail exists, move one piece and prove it again. Designing a board that works is the win.

PARENT / HOST NOTE: Keep the pace unhurried. Praise the plan, not the speed: 'You checked every rule before moving' is the compliment that matters. If a player goes quiet, hand them the bee counter and let them move it — hands first, words follow. Stop whenever the group is happily done; the trail log keeps the score and the favourite board for next time.
