# The Midnight Bakery

*A warm bake-along story that rises one choice at a time*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 5–10 · **Chinese name:** 午夜面包房 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A cozy bake-along story for the quiet end of the day. Each chapter pairs one simple baking step — waking the dough, shaping the stars, waiting while they rise — with one story choice the child makes, so hands and imagination work together. A buddy baker keeps the night shift funny and kind, the pace stays gentle with a calm bedtime mode, and the finished story plus the bake steps completed together are saved in a parent log to read in the morning. No timers, no scores, no rushing — just a warm window, a sleepy oven, and a tale that rises as the dough does.

## Sample content

### Sample · Night One: The Star Buns That Wouldn't Sleep (complete bake-along chapter)

THE MIDNIGHT BAKERY — NIGHT ONE: THE STAR BUNS THAT WOULDN'T SLEEP

Opening (read slowly): When the town clock chimed ten, every shop closed its eyes — except one. At the end of Lantern Lane, a small bakery window glowed gold. Inside, the oven hummed a low, sleepy song, and Alex, the night baker's helper, tied on an apron that was still a little too big.

Miso the sleepy cat was already there, curled on a flour sack. 'I am not sleeping,' Miso said, with one eye open. 'I am guarding the flour. It is a very important job.'

STEP 1 — WAKE THE DOUGH (story choice): On the counter sat a bowl of dough that had been resting all afternoon. It needed waking up gently. Alex could (A) tap the bowl three times and say good evening, or (B) sing the bakery's tiny waking song. The child chooses — and the dough answers differently each way: a tap makes it sigh and stretch; the song makes it bubble once, like a small laugh. Either way is right. The story remembers the choice.

STEP 2 — SHAPE THE STARS: Alex pressed the soft dough flat, just a little, and cut out stars — one big, one small, and one with a wobbly point that Miso declared 'the bravest star, because it tried the hardest.' Counting together, slowly: one star for the window, one for the door, one for anyone who was still awake and feeling a bit lonely. That is the bakery's rule: nobody lonely goes without a warm bun.

STEP 3 — THE WAITING GAME (while the buns rise): Waiting is the hardest baking step, so the bakery plays the Smell Game. Alex closed both eyes. 'I smell warm butter,' said Alex. 'I smell cinnamon,' said Miso, 'and also — I smell that I would like a bun.' Pip the sparrow tapped at the window: 'You smell SLEEPY, Miso!' Everyone laughed, and the dough, hearing happy voices, rose a little higher. In this bakery, kind voices help things rise. That is not magic. That is just how warm kitchens work.

STEP 4 — THE OVEN'S SONG: When the stars turned golden, the oven sang its finished song — three soft dings. Alex used the big wooden peel, very carefully, with grown-up hands helping in the story, and set the star buns on the cooling rack. Steam curled up in the shapes of tiny moons.

ENDING — THE FIRST TASTE AND THE RETELL: The wobbly star bun went to Miso, because brave tries deserve warm endings. Then Alex told the whole night back in three sentences — who came, what they baked, and which choice they liked best — and the bakery wrote it in the Night Log for a parent to read in the morning. The window dimmed. The oven hummed lower. 'Same time tomorrow?' whispered Pip. Alex nodded. There were berry tarts still dreaming in the recipe book, waiting for Night Two.

Parent note: this chapter practiced counting to three slowly, waiting patiently through a rising pause, and retelling a short sequence in order — all inside the story, with no score and no timer shown to the child.
