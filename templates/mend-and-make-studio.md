# The Mend & Make Studio

*Turn worn favourites into kept stories — one visible repair at a time*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Adults · **Chinese name:** 修补手作工坊 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A calm studio for grown-ups who would rather repair a favourite thing than replace it. Bring one worn, torn or tired object — a sweater with a thin elbow, a book with a loose spine, a chipped bowl — and the studio walks you through one honest repair, step by step, with the tools you already own first. Choose whether the fix should disappear or show proudly: visible mending turns a patch into part of the object's story, with contrasting cloth, a stitched grid and a small story tag you write yourself. Nothing is rushed and nothing is faked — every session states its real time, counts its stitches and materials plainly, and ends with care notes so the repair lasts. What's inside: a repair card library for clothing and textiles, wood and everyday objects, paper and books, and ceramics and clay; materials and tools lists that start with what you already own; a visible-mending pattern pack (patches, darning, stitched grids); and a story tag and care card maker. How it works: pick one item and one focus, lay out the short materials list, follow the numbered repair card at your own pace, then finish with care notes and a story tag for the piece.

## Sample content

### Sample Session · Session One: The Blue Sweater's Elbow (complete worked repair)

SESSION CARD — Maker: the studio keeper · Adults · About 45 min · Place: the worktable by the lamp · The rule of the studio: one item, one repair, no rushing.

THE ITEM
A blue wool sweater, worn soft over many winters, with a hole at the left elbow. Measured honestly: the hole is about 2 cm by 2 cm, with the wool thinning in a halo around it. The rest of the sweater is sound — this is a repair, not a rescue.

MATERIALS & TOOLS (from what you already own first)
One scrap of contrasting cloth for the patch — here, mustard cotton from an old shirt; a needle with an eye big enough for the thread; thread in a contrasting colour, one 60 cm length; small sharp scissors; tailor's chalk or a light pencil; 4 pins; an iron if you have one (optional). Nothing on this list needs buying to begin.

PREP — 10 MINUTES
Step 1 — Wash and dry the sweater flat, so the repair is made on the cloth as it will actually live. Step 2 — Cut the patch: the hole is 2 cm across, and a good patch reaches 2 cm beyond the worn area on every side, so cut a square 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 cm by 6 cm. Step 3 — If you have an iron, press the patch flat; if not, smooth it firmly with your palms. Fray check: fold each patch edge under by 5 mm and press or pinch the fold, so no raw edge shows.

MARKING — 5 MINUTES
Step 4 — Lay the sweater flat, sleeve straight, and centre the patch over the hole. Pin it at the four corners. Step 5 — With chalk, mark the stitch grid on the patch: lines 1 cm apart across the 6 cm patch — at 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 cm — that is 7 lines one way, then turn the patch and mark 7 lines the other way. The grid is the pattern and the strength at once: where the lines cross, the old wool and the new cloth are held together.

STITCHING — 20 MINUTES
Step 6 — Thread the needle with the 60 cm length and knot the end. Work running stitch along each chalk line: needle down, needle up, stitches about 5 mm long and 5 mm apart, keeping the line straight by eye rather than by worry. Step 7 — Stitch all 7 lines one way, then all 7 lines the other way. Where a stitch crosses the thin halo around the hole, keep the tension gentle — snug, never puckered. Step 8 — When the grid is done, run one final line of stitching just inside the patch's folded edge, all the way around, to lock the edge down. Finish with two small backstitches and trim the thread to 1 cm. Time check: 14 grid lines plus 1 edge line at a calm pace is the full 20 minutes — if you run long, the session simply runs long; the card never hurries you.

FINISHING — 5 MINUTES
Step 9 — Turn the sleeve and check the inside: the patch should lie flat, with no loops of thread waiting to catch. Snip any loose end longer than 1 cm. Step 10 — Try the sweater on and bend the elbow. The patch should move with the wool, not fight it. If one corner lifts, add three small stitches there — a mend is allowed to be adjusted; that is the point of making it yourself.

STORY TAG & CARE — 5 MINUTES
Write the story tag in your own words — the studio never writes it for you. This session's tag, in the keeper's words: 'Blue sweater, left elbow. Mended at the worktable with mustard cloth from an old shirt. The patch is meant to be seen.' Care notes: wash cool and dry flat; check the grid each winter and re-stitch any line that loosens — five minutes of care keeps the repair for years.

KEEPER'S NOTE: A visible mend is not a flaw with good PR. It is a true sentence about the object: it was worn, it wore out, and somebody decided it was worth keeping.
