# Cloud Shepherd Stories

*Gentle sky stories, herded home one cloud at a time*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 4–8 · **Chinese name:** 云朵牧人故事 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A calm storytelling template where a young cloud shepherd helps gather the day's stray clouds and hears the small story each one carried. Every session follows the same soothing shape — count the clouds, meet them one by one in order, listen to each cloud's tiny tale, then guide them home as the sky dims. Companions are comedy and comfort only, never clues or lessons forced on the child. Parents get a story log of which clouds were met and the child's favourite moment.

## Features

- One cloud at a time — a calm, countable story shape
- Each cloud carries one tiny complete tale
- Shepherd Bram + sparrow Pip: gentle comedy, never clues

## Sample content

### Sample · Evening One: Rowan and the Three Stray Clouds (complete session)

EVENING CARD — Shepherd: Rowan · Ages 5–6 · English · About 10 min · Calm bedtime mode · Companions: Old Shepherd Bram + Pip the sparrow.

THE COUNTING

The sun was folding itself away behind the hill, and the sky was turning the soft colour of apricots. Rowan, the young cloud shepherd, climbed the little grassy rise with a crook that was almost — but not quite — too tall, and looked up.

"Stray clouds," said Old Shepherd Bram, stroking his beard, which had a sparrow in it. The sparrow was Pip. Pip climbed out, shook herself, and said, "I was NOT napping. I was supervising."

Rowan counted slowly, the way shepherds do, pointing the crook at each one. "One… two… three. Three stray clouds tonight, Bram. Three exactly."

"Then we meet them one by one," said Bram, "in order, the way we always do. A cloud at a time, and never all at once."

CLOUD ONE — THE LITTLE ROUND CLOUD

The first cloud was small and round and very pleased with itself, like a dumpling that had learned to fly. It drifted down until it hung just above Rowan's crook.

"I carried a story today," the Little Round Cloud whispered. "A red kite got lost this morning. Its string slipped a small boy's fingers at the top of the windy field, and up it came, and up, until it bumped — very gently — into me. It was frightened, so I carried it all afternoon, slow and low, until the wind showed it the way back down to its own garden. The boy waved. The kite waved. Well. Its tail did."

"Thank you for carrying it kindly," said Rowan.

Pip whispered, "I once carried a kite. It carried me back. We do not discuss it." Bram pretended not to laugh, and failed, quietly, into his beard.

CLOUD TWO — THE LONG RIBBON CLOUD

The second cloud was long and thin, stretched out like a ribbon across the dimming sky. When Rowan lifted the crook, it unrolled itself a little further, proud as a banner.

"My story," it sighed, "is a thirsty garden. All day I floated over a garden whose lettuces had drooped and whose beans were asking, politely at first, for water. So I did the best thing a cloud can do." And as it spoke, the Long Ribbon Cloud let down its rain — gentle rain, soft as a held breath — patter, patter, patter, on the garden far below. Somewhere down in the dark, the lettuces stood up again.

"That was a kind thing to carry," said Rowan. "And a kind way to set it down."

CLOUD THREE — THE SLEEPY GREY CLOUD

The third cloud was grey and soft and could barely keep its edges together. It yawned, and the yawn made a tiny fog.

"My story," it murmured, "is a sleepy fishing boat. It had fished since before the sun was up, and by evening its lamp was blinking, slow, slow. So I hung above it as it chugged home, so it would not feel alone on the grey water. Its lamp blinked thank-you. Twice. Then once more, because it had nearly fallen asleep mid-blink."

"Rest now," said Rowan. "You carried it all the way home. That is enough carrying for one day."

THE HERDING HOME

Then, one by one, in the same order they had met them, Rowan guided the clouds home. First the Little Round Cloud, tucked into the soft fold of sky above the hill. Then the Long Ribbon Cloud, folded once, folded twice, laid gently along the horizon. Last of all the Sleepy Grey Cloud, who was asleep before it arrived, so Rowan and Bram guided it the slowest of all, and Pip flew ahead — very quietly, for once — to hold the sky-door open.

The sky dimmed, scene by scene, the way a lamp is lowered: apricot, then rose, then the deep blue of almost-night. One small star. Then two.

"Three clouds out," said Bram. "Three clouds home. That is a shepherd's whole arithmetic."

"And three stories," said Rowan, "safe for the night."
