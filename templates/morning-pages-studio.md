# The Morning Pages Studio

*Begin the day with three honest pages — no audience, no editing, just clarity*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Adults · **Chinese name:** 晨页写作工坊 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A calm morning writing studio for grown-ups who want to start the day clear-headed. Each session is simple and honest: sit down before the day gets loud, choose one gentle prompt (or none at all), and write by hand or on screen for a set, unhurried time — three pages in spirit, whatever that means at your pace — without editing, re-reading or showing anyone. The studio never grades, counts streaks or shares a word: the pages are private by design, and the only thing you keep is a single keepsake line you choose yourself at the end, plus an optional short reflection on what surfaced. Prompts are deliberately ordinary — what is on your mind, what you noticed yesterday, what today actually needs — because clarity comes from honesty, not cleverness. What's inside: a prompt card library organised by focus (clearing the mind, noticing and gratitude, planning the day gently, working through a question), a timed-but-gentle writing session guide that protects the no-editing rule kindly, a reflection close with three short questions, and a keepsake-line maker that stores only the one line you choose. How it works: pick a focus and a session length, take one prompt card, write without stopping to fix anything, then close with the reflection and your keepsake line — and let the rest of the pages go.

## Sample content

### Sample Session · Morning One: What Is Actually on My Mind (a complete morning pages session)

MORNING CARD — Writer: Alex · Adults · Clearing the mind · One gentle prompt · Unhurried · About 20 min · One-line keepsake. The studio rules, said once and kindly: write without editing, do not re-read as you go, and show the pages to no one. The only thing that leaves this desk is the keepsake line you choose at the end.

1 · ARRIVE (2 minutes)
Sit down before the day gets loud. Put the notebook or screen in front of you, pour something warm, and read the studio rule on the card aloud, quietly: "These pages are for no one. That is what makes them honest." Set the gentle timer for 20 minutes — it will chime once, softly, at the end, and never before. There is no streak to protect and no score to earn. A missed morning is just a missed morning; the studio will be here tomorrow.

2 · THE PROMPT CARD (1 minute)
Today's card, from the Clearing the Mind deck, reads: "What is actually on my mind this morning — not what should be, what is?" Turn the card face-down once you have read it. You do not need to answer it cleverly. You only need to start where you are.

3 · WRITE — PAGE ONE (about 7 minutes)
Write without stopping to fix anything. Spelling, handwriting and sense are all allowed to be untidy. If you stall, write the stall — "I do not know what to write" is a true sentence and it counts. An honest opening, in the studio keeper's hand, might begin like this: "What is on my mind is the kitchen tap that drips, and the email I have been avoiding since Tuesday, and the fact that I slept well and still woke up tired. I keep thinking about the walk I did not take yesterday. I am not worried, exactly. I am full. Like a desk with too many papers on it, none of them urgent, all of them mine." Keep going from there in your own words — the keeper's lines are only a way in, never a model to copy. When page one fills, turn the page without re-reading it.

4 · WRITE — PAGES TWO AND THREE (about 10 minutes)
Somewhere on page two, the obvious things run out, and that is the point. The smaller, truer things arrive underneath: a kindness you forgot to thank someone for, a decision that is simpler than you have been making it, a sentence you actually want to say out loud today. Write those down exactly as they come, even mid-sentence, even twice. On page three, if the prompt has been used up, let the writing wander — a list is allowed, a letter you will never send is allowed, silence written as a row of honest dots is allowed. Stop only when the soft chime sounds. Total writing check: about 7 minutes for page one plus about 10 minutes for pages two and three, inside the 20-minute card with the arrival and prompt minutes — the arithmetic is 2 + 1 + 7 + 10 = 20, and if your hand is slower, three pages in spirit is always enough.

5 · REFLECTION CLOSE (3 questions, short answers)
Close the notebook first. Then answer these three, briefly, on the reflection card — not in the pages:
(1) "What surprised me?" — Keeper's example: "That the avoided email was not the heavy thing. The untaken walk was."
(2) "What is one true sentence from today?" — Keeper's example: "I am not behind. I am full, and full can be emptied one paper at a time."
(3) "What does today actually need — just one thing?" — Keeper's example: "Take the walk first, then send the email. In that order."

6 · THE KEEPSAKE LINE
Choose ONE line — only one — to keep. It can be your true sentence from question two, or any single line from the pages you are willing to carry into the day. The keeper's keepsake for Morning One is: "I am not behind. I am full, and full can be emptied one paper at a time." Write the keepsake on its own card, say the closing line aloud — "The pages stay here. The line comes with me." — and let the rest go unkept and unread. That letting-go is not waste. It is the whole design.
