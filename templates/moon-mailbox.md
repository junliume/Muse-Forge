# The Moon Mailbox

*Write to the Moon tonight. Wake up to a story that answers you.*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 4–8 · **Chinese name:** 月亮信箱 · **Spec version:** 1.1

## What it is

Your child dictates or types one letter to the Moon at bedtime; the next morning (or next bedtime) a reply story arrives that truly answers that letter. The stories form a 12-episode serial season, The Season the Stars Went Dim, with a recurring cast: Luna the Moon Postkeeper, Pip the hedgehog mail-carrier, and the Comet Twins. Every letter and reply is kept in a keepsake Letter & Story Journal that parents can preview and print from the Parent Zone.

## Features

- One letter at bedtime → a reply story that answers it
- 12-episode serial: The Season the Stars Went Dim
- Cast: Luna the Moon Postkeeper, Pip the hedgehog, Comet Twins
- Keepsake Letter & Story Journal — preview & print
- The reply truly answers the letter: it names what the child asked, lost, feared or celebrated — never a generic story with the name swapped in
- A gentle season thread underneath every reply: one dim star is re-lit per episode, so the serial visibly progresses toward its cozy finale
- Letter prompt cards for stuck writers: a question for the Moon, something lost, something kind that happened, a wish for someone else
- Parent preview before the reply is read aloud, with a one-tap option to soften any part the parent wants gentler
- A closing ritual in every episode: the same short goodnight exchange between Pip and the child, so bedtime ends predictably even when the story adventures
- Forge standards: gentle music-box music with big stop button, silent parent-only play timing

## Sample content

### Episode 1 · The Mitten on the Moonlight Train

Sam's letter (dictated at bedtime): “Dear Moon, I lost my red mitten at the park. It was my favorite. If you see it from up there, please tell it I miss it. Love, Sam.”

Episode 1 — The Mitten on the Moonlight Train. That night, Pip the hedgehog mail-carrier tucked Sam's letter into his satchel and climbed the moonbeam stairs. Luna the Moon Postkeeper read it twice, then looked down at the park with her silver telescope. “There,” she said. “Under the third bench, glowing a little, the way loved things do.” The Comet Twins hitched the Moonlight Train to a falling star, and Pip rode down with an empty seat saved beside him. He lifted the red mitten gently — it was cold, and a little lonely, and very glad to be found. All the way home the mitten rode by the window, watching the rooftops go by, and Pip told it, “Sam never stopped missing you, not for one minute.” In the morning the mitten was back on the porch rail, smelling faintly of starlight — and Sam knew the Moon had answered.

### Episode 2 · The Star That Was Afraid of the Dark (excerpt)

Sam's letter (chosen from a prompt card: a wish for someone else): “Dear Moon, my friend at school is scared of the dark. Can you send her a little bit of your light? Not a lot. Just enough. Love, Sam.” That night Pip carried the letter up the moonbeam stairs, and Luna read it three times — once for the words, once for the kindness, and once just to smile at it. “Enough light for a friend,” she said, and she called the smallest of the dim stars, the one hiding behind her own hands. The star whispered that she was not only dim — she was afraid of the dark herself, which is a hard thing for a star to admit. So Luna did not send the star down. She sent her a job instead: to sit in one particular window, in one particular street, and practise shining just a candle's worth — no more, because Sam had said not a lot, just enough. All week the star practised, a little braver each night, and on the seventh night the window glowed the way a night-light glows: small, steady, and exactly enough. Pip closed the episode the way he always does, tapping the mailbox twice. “Letters answered, stars re-lit — one. Goodnight, Sam. The Moon is keeping watch, so you don't have to.”
