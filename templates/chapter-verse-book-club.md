# Chapter & Verse Book Club

*A warm book-club companion that writes the discussion for you*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Adults · **Chinese name:** 章节读书会 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A book-club companion for grown-ups: pick a genre focus, group size and meeting rhythm, and it prepares a complete session — a short original reading, warm-up question, layered discussion questions that move from first impressions to craft to personal connection, a closing round, and a next-book picker. No literary expertise needed; the host gets gentle facilitation notes so quieter members are drawn in and no one dominates. All readings are original short pieces written for the club, never excerpts from copyrighted books.

## Sample content

### Sample Session · The Cartographer of Small Streets (complete session)

SESSION CARD — Host: Alex · Classic club (5–8) · Monthly · Balanced · About 60 min. Tonight's reading is an original short piece written for the club.

THE READING — "The Cartographer of Small Streets"

Mara drew maps for a living, but the maps she loved were the ones nobody paid for. Those she kept in a green cloth notebook, and she worked by one rule, though she never wrote the rule down: a street only earned its page if she had walked it with someone she loved.

On the first page was Willow Lane, drawn the winter her brother taught her to ride a bicycle, his hand hovering — never quite touching — above the saddle. She had marked the exact corner where she stopped wobbling, with a small gold star. On page nine was the market street in the coastal town where her grandmother bought plums and argued, happily and at length, about their price; Mara drew the stall, the striped awning, even the cat asleep on the sacks of rice.

Page twenty-three held the hospital road, walked slowly, many times, the year her friend was ill — and beside its careful lines, in pencil, a note: "We laughed here anyway." Page thirty-one was barely a street at all, just the lane behind the library where, one October evening, someone had taken her hand for the first time and neither of them had said anything about it for a whole block.

The maps were not accurate. Distances bent toward whoever had been beside her. A street walked with her mother stretched long and golden, taking up two pages. A street walked alone did not appear at all, however grand it was — the wide boulevard with the marble fountain had never once made the notebook.

On the last page there was nothing. No street, no star, no note. Visitors who flipped through sometimes asked whether she had run out of streets. Mara always smiled. "That page," she said, "is for a street I haven't walked yet."

WARM-UP (5 min) — Name one street you could draw from memory, corner by corner. Who is walking beside you in your version?
Host note: keep this light and quick — thirty seconds each, no stories yet. The stories will come.

DISCUSSION QUESTIONS

First impressions
1. Which image stayed with you after the last line — the gold star, the cat on the rice sacks, the note "We laughed here anyway," the blank page? Why that one?
Host note: go around the circle once; first impressions are never wrong, so don't let anyone correct anyone else here.
2. Did the ending feel surprising, or inevitable? At what moment did you first sense where Mara's notebook was leading?
Host note: if two members disagree, name it kindly — "We have two readings of the ending" — and let both stand.

Craft
3. Mara's rule — a street only counts if she walked it with someone she loved — is never stated outright until you infer it. How does the piece teach you the rule without announcing it?
Host note: point back to the pattern of examples (brother, grandmother, friend, mother) if the group stalls; listing them in order usually unlocks it.
4. The reading is built from small inventories: a star, an awning, a pencil note. What do these concrete details do that a paragraph explaining Mara's feelings could not?
Host note: this is the analytical heart of the session — give it a full ten minutes, and let silence work before rescuing it.

Personal connection
5. If you kept a notebook like Mara's, which street would you draw first — and who would be beside you in the margin?
Host note: invite the quietest member in by name here; this question is safe to answer briefly, and brief answers deserve full attention.
6. Mara's last page is blank "for a street I haven't walked yet." What blank page are you carrying at the moment — a place, a conversation, a beginning?
Host note: the deepest question comes last on purpose. It is always fine to pass; a passed question still lands.

CLOSING ROUND (5 min) — One sentence each, no explanations needed: name a street — real, remembered, or still hoped for — that you would put on your own last-but-one page.

NEXT-BOOK PICKER — Tonight's focus: Literary fiction + Memoir & essays. Because this reading blended an invented life with a memoir's tenderness, the picker suggests alternating: next month choose a slim memoir about a single place — one street, one house, one harbour town — and read it in the same spirit. If the group votes fiction instead, pick a short novel where a journey or a remembered town does the emotional work. Either way, keep it under 250 pages so the monthly rhythm stays kind.
