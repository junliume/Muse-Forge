# The Kite Tail Tales

*Cozy sky stories tied one ribbon at a time*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 5–9 · **Chinese name:** 风筝尾巴故事集 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A gentle co-created storytelling template where each story is a kite: the child chooses a kite, Muse offers one slow opening line, and together they tie a new ribbon — one short story beat — onto the tail for every turn. Ribbons are colour-coded by beat (beginning, wish, gentle trouble, kind idea, ending) so children feel story shape without a lesson. The kite sprite, a paper crane, and a wind mouse appear only for warmth and humour; they never decide the plot. The child always leads, Muse repeats the child's line back to confirm before offering the next one, pace stays slow and patient, and every session ends with the full kite read back as one smooth story plus a calm closing line.

## Sample content

### Sample · The Kite That Collected a Song (complete co-created story)

SESSION CARD — Storyteller: Alex · Ages 6–7 · English · About 20 min · Theme: Sky and wind · Crew: the whole crew · Pace: Gentle · Ending: Full read-back + calm close.

HOW THE KITE WORKS — THE FIVE RIBBONS
Every story here is a kite, and every kite tail grows one ribbon at a time. Each colour means one part of the story, so you can watch the story's shape flying behind it:
• ORANGE ribbon — the Beginning: where we are, and what starts moving.
• YELLOW ribbon — the Wish: what the hero hopes for.
• ROSE ribbon — the Gentle Trouble: a small, kind-sized problem. Never scary, and never for long.
• GREEN ribbon — the Kind Idea: the gentle thought that helps.
• BLUE ribbon — the Ending: everything lands, calm and complete.
Muse says one slow line. Then it is your turn. Muse always repeats your line back, exactly as you said it, so you can hear that it is truly yours — and only then do we tie on the next ribbon together. The kite sprite, the paper crane and the wind mouse come along only to keep us company and make us smile. They never decide what happens. You do.

CHOOSING THE KITE
Muse: "Alex, behind the garden gate this morning there are three kites sleeping in the long grass: a red one shaped like a leaf, a blue one shaped like a fish, and a small orange one shaped like a slice of toast with the corners tucked in. Which kite is yours today?"
Alex: "The orange one. The toast one."
Muse: "The orange one — the toast one. It is yours. And look: the kite sprite is waking up on its tail, rubbing her eyes. She is no bigger than a teacup, and she yawns in ribbons. She says good morning, and that she will hold the ribbons and nothing more, because holding ribbons is a sprite's whole job."

RIBBON ONE — BEGINNING (orange)
Muse: "On a breezy morning, behind the garden gate, Alex found a small orange kite — and the very moment the string touched Alex's hand, the kite tugged, gently, politely, but quite firmly, toward the hill."
Muse: "That is our beginning, Alex, and the orange ribbon is tied. The kite is tugging toward the hill. What does Alex do?"
Alex: "Alex follows it. Slowly. Holding the string with both hands."
Muse: "Alex follows it — slowly, holding the string with both hands. Yes, just like that. Up the garden path you go, past the apple tree, and the kite climbs a little higher with every step, as if it is showing you the way on purpose."

RIBBON TWO — THE WISH (yellow)
Muse: "At the top of the hill the kite pauses and turns its orange face toward the rooftops of the village below. Something up here sounds different, Alex. When the wind crosses the rooftops it does not just blow. It almost — almost — sings. The yellow ribbon is the wish ribbon. What does Alex wish for, up here on the hill?"
Alex: "Alex wishes to hear what the wind sings over the rooftops."
Muse: "Alex wishes to hear what the wind sings over the rooftops — that is the wish, exactly as you said it, and the yellow ribbon is tied. Now listen with me, Alex. If we are very quiet… there. A low note over the baker's chimney. A high, thin note over the church. The wind is warming up, the way singers do."
The paper crane, who has been gliding in slow circles pretending not to listen, drifts a little closer. Cranes are shy about songs. This one folds her paper neck down and whispers, "I only hum when nobody is listening" — and then hums anyway, two notes, very quietly.

RIBBON THREE — GENTLE TROUBLE (rose)
Muse: "But oh — gently, gently — a slow puff of wind turns the kite in a circle, and the string, just for a moment, is not watching where it goes. It loops, softly and kindly, not in a cross way at all, around the top branch of the apple tree. The kite is not stuck fast. It is only… tangled, the way shoelaces get tangled. That is the rose ribbon, Alex: a gentle trouble."
Alex: "Can the paper crane fly up and untie it?"
Muse: "Can the paper crane fly up and untie it — a kind thought, Alex. Let us ask her. Crane? The string is looped around the apple tree's top branch. Could your paper beak reach it?"
The crane flaps up, brave and wobbly, all the way to the top branch. She reaches. She stretches. She is one whole centimetre too short. So she comes down very slowly, lands on the fence, and — because she is embarrassed — folds herself into a smaller crane. Then, quieter still, into a smaller one. The kite sprite pats her gently with one ribbon until she unfolds herself back to her proper size. Some troubles, Alex, are not crane-shaped troubles.

RIBBON FOUR — THE KIND IDEA (green)
Muse: "The green ribbon is for the kind idea — the gentle thought that helps. Down by the garden bench, somebody small has been listening. The wind mouse is sitting on the bench with his whiskers in the breeze. Wind mice speak in very short sentences, Alex, because the wind keeps blowing the ends of their words away. Listen."
Wind mouse: "Leaves are humming."
Wind mouse: "Hum with them."
Wind mouse: "Kite will dance free."
Muse: "Do you hear his idea, Alex? The leaves of the apple tree are humming in the wind — the same tune, the very beginning of the rooftop song. The wind mouse thinks that if someone hums along with the leaves, the kite will sway in time, and sway loose. But who will hum, Alex — and where will they stand to hum?"
Alex: "Alex stands on the garden bench and hums the tune the leaves are making."
Muse: "Alex stands on the garden bench and hums the tune the leaves are making — word for word, Alex, that is the idea, and the green ribbon is tied. Up you climb, onto the bench, holding the string with both hands, just like before. Now hum with me. Hmm… hmm… The leaves take up the tune. The branches sway — left, right — keeping time. And the kite, high above the apple tree, begins to sway too — left, right — dancing in its sleep… until the loop of string slides, slips, and sighs itself free."

RIBBON FIVE — THE ENDING (blue)
Muse: "The blue ribbon is the last one, Alex — the ending ribbon, and endings belong to the storyteller. The kite is free. The wind is singing over the rooftops. Tell me how this story lands."
Alex: "The kite flies all the way up, and Alex hears the whole song, and then Alex hums it all the way home."
Muse: "The kite flies all the way up, and Alex hears the whole song, and then Alex hums it all the way home. Yes. That is the ending, exactly. So the kite climbs — up past the crane, up past the swallows, up to where the clouds keep their quiet — and the wind, pleased to finally have a listener, sings the whole rooftop song: low over the baker's chimney, high over the church, soft over the school, and one long golden note over your own house, Alex — the note it was saving. And you hum it all the way home, down the hill, past the apple tree, along the garden path — and the song stays in your ears the way a ribbon stays on a tail."

THE FULL STORY — READ BACK, ALL FIVE RIBBONS FLYING
Muse: "Now I will read the whole kite back to you, Alex, as one smooth story — your story, every ribbon in its place. Settle in, and just listen."
"On a breezy morning, behind the garden gate, Alex found a small orange kite shaped like a slice of toast with the corners tucked in. The moment the string touched Alex's hand, the kite tugged — gently, politely, but quite firmly — toward the hill, and Alex followed it slowly, holding the string with both hands. At the top of the hill the kite turned its orange face to the rooftops below, and Alex wished, with a whole heart, to hear what the wind sings over the rooftops. Then a slow puff of wind turned the kite in a circle, and its string looped softly around the top branch of the apple tree — tangled, the way shoelaces get tangled. The paper crane flew up bravely to untie it, but she was one whole centimetre too short, and she folded herself smaller and smaller with embarrassment until the kite sprite patted her back to size. That was when the wind mouse, who speaks in very short sentences, pointed out that the apple leaves were already humming the rooftop tune. So Alex stood on the garden bench and hummed with the leaves, the branches swayed in time, and the kite swayed with them until the loop of string slid free. Free at last, the kite climbed high above the village, and the wind sang its whole song — low over the baker's chimney, high over the church, and one long golden note over Alex's own house. And Alex hummed the song all the way home, and hung the kite by the door, where it sleeps tonight with all five ribbons showing: orange, yellow, rose, green and blue."

THE CALM CLOSE
Muse: "Five ribbons, Alex: orange, yellow, rose, green and blue — a whole story, and every beat of it yours. The kite is hung by the door, the crane is asleep in her proper size, and the wind mouse has said his last three words of the day. Tomorrow there will be another breezy morning, and another kite waiting behind the gate — perhaps a red leaf, perhaps a blue fish. Sleep well, storyteller. And when you wake, you can tell me: which colour kite shall we choose tomorrow?"
