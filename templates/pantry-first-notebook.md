# The Pantry-First Notebook

*A calm kitchen notebook that cooks from what you already have — and wastes less of it*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Chinese name:** 储藏室先行笔记 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A quiet notebook for the shelf, jar, and freezer you already own. Instead of starting each week from a shopping list, the Pantry-First Notebook starts from an honest inventory: what is in the cupboard, what is in the freezer, and what needs using first. Each item gets a simple "use-first" flag when it is opened, thawing, or simply old enough to deserve attention, and the week's meal ideas are drafted from the flagged shelf outward — a jar of beans and a heel of cheese become the plan, not the afterthought. The notebook keeps three gentle lists (pantry, freezer, use-first) and one weekly page where meals are sketched against what is on hand, with the short shopping list that remains written last and kept short on purpose. Nothing is scored and nothing is shamed: a tin used six months late is a small victory recorded in the same tone as everything else.

## Features

- Three living lists — pantry, freezer, and use-first — each item with a plain note on when it was opened or frozen
- Use-first flags that surface on their own: opened jars, thawing portions, and the oldest tin in the cupboard rise to the top
- A weekly meal-sketch page that starts from flagged items and builds outward, leaving gaps honestly blank
- The short list written last: after the week's meals are sketched from stock, only the true gaps become the shopping list
- A "jar maths" helper for dried goods — how many portions are really left in the big bag of lentils
- A monthly shelf sweep: one calm page that walks shelf by shelf and retires what is truly past keeping, without commentary
- A substitutions corner: when a meal idea wants one missing thing, the notebook suggests what the shelf already offers instead
- No calorie counts, no waste scores, no streaks — the only record kept is what got used and what got planned

## What's included

## At a glance

- The use-first shelf this week: half a jar of white beans, thawing chicken stock, and a bag of spinach with frost on it — three meals start right there
- Jar maths: the 1 kg bag of red lentils, used twice, holds about six portions — enough for two soups and a dal, not the four meals it was mentally booked for
- The shopping list after the sketch: eggs, lemons, and fresh herbs. Everything else was already home.

## How it works

1. Stocktake once, briefly: walk the cupboard and freezer and write down what is actually there, with opened or frozen dates where known
2. Flag use-first items — opened, thawing, or oldest — and let them rise to the top of the week
3. Sketch the week's meals from the flagged shelf outward; leave a gap blank rather than inventing a meal
4. Write the short list last: only the gaps become shopping
5. Once a month, walk the shelves with the sweep page and quietly retire what is past keeping

## Sample content

### Sample Week · The Week the Beans Led (complete worked week page)

THE USE-FIRST SHELF ON MONDAY. Half a jar of white beans (opened nine days ago), a tub of chicken stock thawing since Sunday, spinach with frost on the bag, four eggs, a heel of hard cheese, and the oldest tin in the cupboard: chopped tomatoes, bought for a recipe nobody now remembers. Nothing here is wasted yet. Everything here goes first.

THE SKETCH, BUILT OUTWARD. Monday: white bean soup — the beans, the thawing stock, an onion from the basket, the cheese heel grated over the top. Three flagged items used in one pot. Tuesday: the frosty spinach folded into eggs — a spinach frittata with the last of a bag of frozen peas for company. Wednesday: left blank on purpose; there will be soup left, and the notebook does not invent meals to fill a grid. Thursday: the oldest tin finally earns its place — tomatoes, the big bag of red lentils (jar maths says six portions remain, this uses two), cumin from the spice shelf: a plain dal-and-tomato pot that makes Friday's lunch as well. The weekend is left open for cooking what the market suggests, which is the notebook's favourite kind of plan.

THE SHORT LIST, WRITTEN LAST. Eggs (the frittata took the last four), one lemon, fresh coriander if it looks alive. That is the whole list. Everything else the week needed was already home, most of it quietly waiting to be flagged.

THE SWEEP NOTE, SAME WEEK. One jar of jam, unopened, eighteen months past its date, retired without ceremony and recorded in one line: "Jam, retired." The notebook keeps no score for this. The shelf is lighter, the list is shorter, and the beans — the beans led the week, exactly as flagged.

NOTEBOOK PROMISE: look at the shelf first, flag what needs using, sketch from what you have, and let the shopping list be whatever is left. It will usually be short.
