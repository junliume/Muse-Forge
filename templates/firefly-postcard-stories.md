# Firefly Postcard Stories

*Tiny stories mailed from a summer night*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 5–9 · **Chinese name:** 萤火虫明信片故事 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A gentle storytelling template where each session is a chain of three picture-postcards from a firefly mail carrier named Pippa. The child picks a destination, a stamp, and a secret the firefly is carrying, then hears or reads a tiny three-part story — Arrival, The Small Problem, The Glow Home — and finishes by dictating or choosing the one sentence Pippa writes on the back of the last postcard. How it works: pick the form options, read the three postcards in order, pause at the marked Pause & Predict moment, then complete the final postcard sentence together. No rewards, no speed — just calm, bedtime-friendly pacing. What's inside: a story engine that builds three-postcard chains, Pippa and her postcard stamps, Pause & Predict prompts, and a final-sentence keepsake log where parents can reread every finished postcard sentence.

## Sample content

### Sample · The Night Pippa Mailed the Moon a Postcard

POSTCARD 1 — ARRIVAL
At dusk, when the willow post office turned its lamps down to a honey glow, Pippa the firefly tucked a blank postcard under her wing and lifted off into the violet air. The postcard was addressed in silver pencil to THE MOON, and it smelled faintly of river mint, because at the willow post office every address is read by smell before it is read by eye.

"You'll want the high road tonight," said Milo the moth, who read addresses by smell and had never once been lost. He pressed his feathery feelers to the postcard, sniffed twice, and nodded. "Moon-mail. Smells of cold light and faraway. Straight up, past the pine tops — and mind the river fog on your way across."

"Thank you, Milo," said Pippa, and her light blinked once, which is how fireflies say please and thank you at the same time. She rose above the willows, above the sleeping boats, until the whole valley lay below her like a dark green quilt stitched with window-lights, and the river ran through it like a ribbon of black glass.

POSTCARD 2 — THE SMALL PROBLEM
But the river had other plans. A thick white fog had rolled off the water and covered the stepping-stones — the very stones Pippa used to find her way across: one glow, rest, one glow, rest. She hovered above the bank and blinked as hard as she could, but one small light in a whole sky of fog is like one word whispered in a very large room. "Oh," she said softly. "I am too small for this much fog."

⏸ PAUSE & PREDICT: Pippa's light is too small alone, and the fog has hidden her path. What do you think she will do? (Grown-ups: wait for every guess to be finished — in this story there are no wrong guesses, only untested ones.)

Here is what she did. She flew down low, close to the black shining water, and called, "Striders? Are you awake?" The water striders came skating out of the reeds, long-legged and sleepy-eyed. "We cannot glow," said the oldest strider, "but we can answer." So Pippa blinked — once, twice — and the striders tapped the water back, ring by ring, in a line all the way across the river: blink, tap-tap; blink, tap-tap; a dotted path of light and ripples. Pippa followed the dotted line stone by stone, glow by glow, and the fog could not hide a path that was being made at the very moment she flew it.

POSTCARD 3 — THE GLOW HOME
Above the far bank the air cleared all at once, the way it does just after rain. Pippa climbed past the pine tops to the Moon's round white mailbox, which hangs on the edge of the night, and slid the postcard carefully in. "Special delivery," she whispered. "One blank postcard, carried all the way."

The Moon — who that night was only a slim silver crescent — read the address, turned the postcard over, and smiled a small, bright smile. "It is blank," said the Moon, "because tonight the real message is the secret you carried in your heart the whole way here. Say it for me, little carrier, and I will write it down exactly." So Pippa glowed steady and warm, and spoke the brave sentence she had been carrying — her secret cargo — and the Moon wrote it on the back of the postcard in silver pencil, word for word, and underneath added one line of her own: "A light does not have to be big to show the way home."

Then Pippa flew home by strider-light and firefly-light together, and the dotted path winked out behind her, ring by ring, like a story being closed gently for the night.

THE CHILD'S FINAL SENTENCE (completed in full, as the reader's worked example): "Dear Moon, you do not have to be full to be bright."

PARENT KEEPSAKE NOTE: This sentence is saved to the keepsake log with tonight's destination (The Moon), the stamp (Firefly glow) and the secret cargo (A brave sentence), so you can reread every finished postcard sentence in order — a small archive of the things your child wanted the night to know.
