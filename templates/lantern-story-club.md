# The Lantern Story Club

*Stories that glow, one lantern at a time*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 5–10 · **Chinese name:** 灯笼故事会 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

At dusk the Lantern Story Club meets in a quiet garden. Each paper lantern holds a story seed — a sound, a colour, and a small kind deed. Members choose a lantern together, light it (pretend is fine), and tell a story in gentle rounds: one member offers a line, the next adds what happened next, and the lantern keeper asks one kind question before the story moves on. No one has to perform and no story is graded. Short tales finish in one sitting; longer tales save their lantern so the club can relight it next time exactly where they left off.

## Features

- One lantern, one complete story — seeds keep tales focused and finishable
- Gentle rounds: line, next line, one kind question — never a test
- Save a half-told tale and relight its lantern next session
- Calm dusk pacing with a soft close-down ritual for bedtime

## How it works

1. Pick a lantern — choose a seed card with a sound, a colour, and a kind deed.
2. Set the style — pick the telling style, pace, and session length for tonight.
3. Tell it in rounds — a line, the next line, then the keeper's one kind question.
4. Close or save — finish with the close-down ritual, or save the lantern to relight later.

## Sample content

### Sample · The Lantern That Hummed (a complete club tale)

THE LANTERN SEED — Sound: a soft humming. Colour: apricot gold. Kind deed: carry someone's heavy basket.
ROUND ONE: At dusk in the moonlit garden, Alex chose the apricot lantern from its hook. The moment it was lit, it began to hum — a soft, low hum, like a kettle remembering a song. The firefly guides explained: this lantern hums whenever someone nearby is too shy to ask for help.
ROUND TWO: Alex followed the hum past the fern bed to the plum tree, where Mina the moth was struggling with a basket of fallen plums, far too heavy for one small pair of wings. “I can manage,” Mina whispered, though the basket had not moved an inch. Alex lifted the heavy end without a fuss. “Two carriers make one light basket,” Alex said.
ROUND THREE: Mina fluttered ahead, showing Alex the path of dry stepping stones across the damp grass, so no feet got wet and no plums got muddy. Together they carried the basket all the way to the garden gate, safe and dry.
THE KEEPER'S KIND QUESTION: The hedgehog keeper shuffled over, brushed a plum leaf off his nose, and asked: “What did helping feel like from the other side?” Alex thought for a while. “From Mina's side, maybe it felt like not being alone with a heavy thing.”
THE CLOSE: As the last plum was handed over, the lantern's hum softened and settled into a warm, steady glow. Then came the club's close-down ritual: three slow breaths, a thank-you whispered to the lantern, and the lantern hung back on its hook for the night.
(Finished in one session — this tale's lantern is marked complete.)
