# The Estimation Station

*Learn to guess smart — round, estimate, and check if an answer makes sense*

**Category:** Education · **Ages:** Ages 6–9 · **Chinese name:** 估算小站 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A hands-on estimation workshop where learners round numbers, estimate sums and differences before calculating, and learn the most useful check in maths: does this answer make sense? Every lesson starts with a real jar, shelf or shopping basket to estimate, teaches one clear rounding rule with a number line, then practises estimate-first problem solving — estimate, calculate exactly, compare — so learners build number sense instead of guessing wildly. Difficulty and number range adapt to the age band, hints are visual, and nothing is timed.

## Features

- Estimate first, then calculate — every problem in that order
- Rounding taught on a visible number line
- Reasonable-answer checks: is 812 a sensible answer for 48 + 51?
- Real objects to estimate: jars, shelves, baskets and crowds
- No timers, no speed scores — number sense grows calmly

## What's included

- Estimation lesson engine

## At a glance

- A jar of marbles and one question: about how many — 20, 50 or 100?
- The number-line track: 47 sits between 40 and 50, closer to 50 — so 47 rounds to 50
- Estimate, calculate, compare: 48 + 32 is about 50 + 30 = 80 — and the exact answer really is 80

## How it works

1. Look at a real jar, shelf or basket and make a smart estimate first
2. Learn one rounding rule on the number-line track
3. Estimate, then calculate exactly, then compare the two
4. Run the reasonable-answer check: does this answer make sense?

## Sample content

### Sample Lesson · Round It at the Station: Rounding to Tens and Estimating Sums (complete worked lesson)

WELCOME TO THE STATION. At the Estimation Station, trains do not carry exact answers first — they carry good guesses. A good guess in maths has a name: an estimate. Today we learn the station's one big rule and use it six times.

THE RULE — ROUND TO THE NEAREST TEN. Put the number on the number line. 47 sits between 40 and 50. Which ten is it closer to? Count: 47 is 7 steps from 40 and only 3 steps from 50, so 47 rounds UP to 50. Numbers ending in 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9 round up; numbers ending in 1, 2, 3 or 4 round down; a number ending in exactly 5 rounds up. Try the rule: 32 rounds DOWN to 30 (2 steps from 30, 8 from 40). 68 rounds UP to 70. 55 rounds UP to 60.

ESTIMATE A SUM. Problem: 48 + 32. Step 1, round each number: 48 rounds to 50, 32 rounds to 30. Step 2, add the round numbers, which is easy: 50 + 30 = 80. So 48 + 32 is ABOUT 80. Step 3, check exactly: 48 + 32 = 80. The estimate was perfect.

A SECOND SUM. 127 + 89. Round to the nearest ten: 127 rounds to 130, 89 rounds to 90. Estimate: 130 + 90 = 220. Exact: 127 + 89 = 216. Is 216 reasonable? Yes — it is close to our estimate of 220, so the answer makes sense.

ESTIMATE A DIFFERENCE. 63 − 28. Round: 63 rounds to 60, 28 rounds to 30. Estimate: 60 − 30 = 30. Exact: 63 − 28 = 35. Close to 30, so it is reasonable.

THE REASONABLE CHECK. A learner writes 48 + 51 = 812. Use the station check before anything else: 48 rounds to 50 and 51 rounds to 50, so the answer should be about 100. 812 is nowhere near 100, so it cannot be right — a place-value slip. The exact answer is 99. Estimating caught the mistake without redoing the whole sum.

YOUR TURN (answers in brackets): 1) Round 74 to the nearest ten. [70 — 74 is 4 steps from 70 and 6 from 80, so it rounds down.] 2) Estimate 56 + 39, then calculate exactly. [Estimate: 60 + 40 = 100. Exact: 95 — close to 100, reasonable.] 3) Estimate 92 − 47, then calculate exactly. [Estimate: 90 − 50 = 40. Exact: 45 — close to 40, reasonable.] 4) The jar holds about 50 marbles and you add about 30 more. About how many now? [About 80.] 5) Is 205 − 98 = 207 reasonable? [No. Estimate: 200 − 100 = 100, so the answer should be near 100. Exact: 107.] 6) Round 145 to the nearest hundred. [100 — 145 is closer to 100 than to 200.]

STATION PROMISE: estimate first, calculate second, compare always. A learner who estimates is never fooled by a silly answer.
