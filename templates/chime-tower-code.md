# The Chime Tower Code

*A bell-and-lantern logic game where every clue counts*

**Category:** Games · **Ages:** Ages 7–11 · **Chinese name:** 钟楼密码 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A clue-by-clue logic game set in a friendly bell tower: players order five bells by pitch and line up four lanterns by position, using only written clues — no guessing allowed, because every puzzle in the tower has exactly one solution and the game proves it. Players cross off what a clue rules out, keep what it forces, and check the finished order against every clue before the tower chimes. Includes a make-your-own mode where players swap roles and must prove their own puzzle has a single answer before anyone plays it. No timers, no elimination — a wrong order just sends you back to the clues.

## Features

- Every puzzle has exactly one solution — and the game proves it
- Cross-off clue checking: rule out, force, then verify
- Two puzzle families: bell pitch orders and lantern line-ups
- Make-your-own mode: build a puzzle, prove it is fair, then swap
- Helper hints point at a clue — never give the order away

## What's included

- Chime Tower puzzle board & bell cards
- Worked first session with two fully solved puzzles
- Clue-checking grid (rule out / force / verify)
- Make-your-own puzzle maker with fairness proof

## At a glance

- Five bells, four clues, one true order — Brass, Silver, Wood, Iron, Copper
- Four lanterns in a row: one extra clue turns two answers into exactly one
- Make your own puzzle, prove it has a single solution, then watch a friend solve it

## How it works

1. Read every clue aloud before touching a card — the clues are the whole game
2. Place cards only when a clue forces a spot; cross off every spot a clue rules out
3. Before the tower chimes, check the finished order against every single clue

## Sample content

### Sample Session · Two Puzzles, Fully Solved (the five bells and the four lanterns)

THE CHIME TOWER CODE — a complete first session (about 25 minutes)

YOU NEED: five bell cards (Brass, Silver, Wood, Iron, Copper), four lantern cards (Red, Blue, Green, Yellow), and a pencil for crossing off. One player is the Tower Keeper and reads the clues aloud; the other is the Solver. Swap roles for Puzzle 2. The golden rule: never guess a card into a spot — a card moves only when a clue forces it or every other spot is crossed off.

PUZZLE 1 — THE FIVE BELLS, HIGHEST PITCH TO LOWEST. Clues: (1) Silver sounds higher than Iron, but lower than Brass. (2) Copper is the lowest bell of all. (3) Wood sounds higher than Iron, but lower than Silver.
SOLVE IT STEP BY STEP. From clue 2, Copper is forced into spot 5 (lowest) — write it in. From clue 1, Brass is above Silver and Silver is above Iron, so those three must fall in the order Brass, then Silver, then Iron, top to bottom. From clue 3, Wood sits between Silver and Iron: higher than Iron, lower than Silver. So Wood slots in under Silver and above Iron. Now read the tower from top to bottom: Brass (1, highest), Silver (2), Wood (3), Iron (4), Copper (5, lowest). CHECK EVERY CLUE: Silver higher than Iron — yes, 2 is above 4. Silver lower than Brass — yes, 2 is below 1. Copper lowest — yes, spot 5. Wood higher than Iron — yes, 3 is above 4. Wood lower than Silver — yes, 3 is below 2. Every clue true, and no other order can work: the clues chain all five bells into one line, Brass over Silver over Wood over Iron over Copper, so this is the ONLY solution.

PUZZLE 2 — THE FOUR LANTERNS, LEFT TO RIGHT. Clues: (1) Green hangs immediately to the right of Blue. (2) Red hangs at an end. (3) Yellow does not hang next to Red. (4) Yellow hangs somewhere to the right of Blue.
SOLVE IT STEP BY STEP. Clue 1 means Blue and Green are a locked pair, in that order, so the pair can start at spot 1, 2 or 3 — try each. Pair at 1-2 (Blue, Green): the free spots are 3 and 4, and Red must be at an end, so Red is 4 and Yellow is 3 — but then Yellow is next to Red, breaking clue 3. Cross it off. Pair at 3-4 (Blue at 3, Green at 4): Red at an end means Red is 1 and Yellow is 2 — but Yellow is then to the LEFT of Blue, breaking clue 4. Cross it off. Pair at 2-3 (Blue at 2, Green at 3): the ends 1 and 4 are free for Red and Yellow. Yellow must be right of Blue (clue 4), so Yellow is 4 and Red is 1. CHECK EVERY CLUE: Green immediately right of Blue — spots 3 and 2, yes. Red at an end — spot 1, yes. Yellow not next to Red — spots 4 and 1 are far apart, yes. Yellow right of Blue — 4 is right of 2, yes. The line-up is Red, Blue, Green, Yellow — and because the other two pair positions were crossed off, it is the ONLY solution. Notice what clue 4 did: without it, Red-Blue-Green-Yellow and Yellow-Blue-Green-Red would BOTH work, and the puzzle would be unfair. A fair puzzle needs exactly one answer.

MAKE YOUR OWN (10 minutes): the Solver becomes Keeper. Pick four cards, secretly fix one order, then write clues one at a time. After each clue, test honestly: could a different order still fit every clue so far? Stop adding clues only when exactly one order survives — then hand the puzzle over and watch it being solved. If two orders survive, the puzzle is not broken, it is unfinished: add the one clue that separates them.

TOWER RULES TO KEEP: read all clues before placing any card; a card placed by force is written in ink, a card placed by a hunch stays in pencil; before the tower chimes, the Keeper reads every clue once more and the Solver points at where each clue is satisfied. Wrong order? No penalty — go back to the clue that fails and let it teach you.
