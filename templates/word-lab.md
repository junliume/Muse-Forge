# Word Lab: Vocabulary & Reading Quest

*Learn powerful words in real stories — then use them like a writer.*

**Category:** Education · **Also listed under:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 6–10 · **Chinese name:** 词汇实验室 · **Spec version:** 1.1

## What it is

Tier 2 vocabulary learned the way strong readers actually learn it: each word is met at least 3 times inside real story contexts, predicted from clues before any definition appears, then produced in the child's own sentence by hold-to-talk. Every chapter ends with a recorded retell for parents, and review follows a D+1/3/7 spaced schedule. No speed rewards, no rankings — the win is using the word like a writer.

## Features

- Tier 2 words met 3+ times in story contexts
- Predict-from-clues before any definition
- Own-sentence production by hold-to-talk
- Recorded retell for parents every chapter
- Spaced review D+1/3/7 · no speed rewards or rankings
- Word webs: each new word grows a small web of near-synonyms, opposites, and word-family forms (discover → discovery, enormous → enormously), so words arrive in families, not alone
- Word-in-the-wild log: the learner can flag a chapter word spotted in another book, a sign, or conversation — spotted words graduate early from the review queue
- Two-step definitions: the learner first picks the best kid-friendly meaning, then hears the word used by a second character in a different setting before the chapter moves on
- Writer's-choice prompts at chapter end: rewrite one story sentence swapping in a chapter word, or deliberately choose the plainer word and say why — both choices are praised, for different reasons
- Mixed review chapters every fourth chapter: no new words, only returning words woven into one fresh story, so review never feels like a test

## Sample content

### Chapter 1 · The Lighthouse Picnic (enormous / whisper / discover)

The story opens: “Mina carried the enormous picnic basket down the cliff path. It was bigger than her little brother. It was bigger than the dog.” Guided exercise 1 — Predict from clues: “The basket is enormous. Which clue tells you enormous means very big?” (It is compared to the brother and the dog.) Exercise 2 — Word in the wild: “Grandpa had to whisper, because the baby gulls were asleep” — what happens to a voice when someone whispers? Exercise 3 — Notice the pattern: in “Under the loose stone, Mina would discover…”, the story pauses — what do you predict she discovers, and which words made you think so? Exercise 4 — Say it yourself (hold-to-talk): use discover in a sentence about your own kitchen. Exercise 5 — Retell: tell the chapter back in your own words, using at least two of this chapter's words; the recording goes to your parent. Method taught: meet the word → predict from clues → check in a new context → say it yourself → meet it again on D+1, D+3, D+7.

### Chapter 2 · The Night Train Window (gleam / drowsy / journey)

The story opens: “The train left the city as the lamps began to gleam. Mina pressed her forehead to the cold window and watched each light smear into a golden ribbon.” Guided exercise 1 — Predict from clues: “The lamps began to gleam. Is gleam about light, sound, or smell — and which words in the sentence prove it?” (Light: lamps, light, golden.) Exercise 2 — Word web: gleam's family — a gleam (the light itself), gleaming (the lamp right now), and its plainer cousin shine. Which one would you pick for the sun at noon, and why is gleam the better word for a small lamp at dusk? Exercise 3 — Second context: “Far below, one window still gleamed in the signal cottage.” Same word, new place — what is gleaming now? Exercise 4 — Word in the wild: the companion asks, “Where could a light gleam in our own street? Tell me one place, and I will write it in the spotting log.” Exercise 5 — Writer's choice: the story's next sentence reads, “Mina felt tired as the carriage rocked.” Rewrite it using drowsy — or keep tired and explain your choice. Both answers earn a star; the explanation is the exercise. Exercise 6 — Retell with a returning word: tell the chapter back using gleam, drowsy, and one word from Chapter 1 (enormous, whisper, or discover); the recording goes to your parent, and the Chapter 1 word counts as its D+3 review without a single flashcard. Method in action: new words met three times, old words returning inside new stories, and every definition earned from clues before it is ever handed over.
