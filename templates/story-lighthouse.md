# Story Lighthouse

*A calm harbour-light keeper who turns each night into a story*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 4–9 · **Chinese name:** 故事灯塔 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A gentle storytelling keeper for calm evenings. Each night, the lighthouse keeper lights the lamp, counts three bays across the harbour, and tells one complete story that travels from bay to bay — beginning at the Harbour Wall, pausing at the Fog Bell Buoy, and ending safely at Gull Rock. Your child chooses the theme and a companion, listens or joins in at the keeper's pauses, and always reaches a settled, sleepy ending in the same night. No cliffhangers, no scores, and no rushing — just a steady light, a kind voice, and a story that comes home.

## Features

- One complete story per night — beginning, middle, and a settled ending
- Three bays every time: Harbour Wall, Fog Bell Buoy, Gull Rock
- Child joins at gentle pauses — a sound, a guess, or one short line
- Calm bedtime mode: slower pace, softer beam, no cliffhangers
- Parent night log: theme, companion, and the child's favourite line

## What's included

## Sample content

### Sample · Night One: Milo and the Fog Bell (complete story)

NIGHT ONE: MILO AND THE FOG BELL — told by Keeper Noor, with Pico the gull and Biscuit the harbour cat.

BAY 1 — THE HARBOUR WALL. Keeper Noor lit the lamp at dusk and said, 'One light, three bays, one story safely home.' On the steps, Biscuit the harbour cat curled around a warm stone. Above the rail, Pico the gull called, 'Evening, evening!' Then a small voice came from the Harbour Wall. It was Milo, the youngest harbour mouse, holding a tiny brass fog bell with both paws. 'Keeper Noor,' said Milo, 'the fog is coming early tonight. If I ring my bell, will the little boats hear it?' Keeper Noor knelt down. 'Ring it once here, Milo, soft and clear, and let us listen together.' Milo rang the bell once — ding — and from the dark water a fishing boat blinked its lamp twice: we hear you. [Keeper's pause: child makes the soft bell sound — ding.]

BAY 2 — THE FOG BELL BUOY. The beam turned slowly to the second bay, where the old Fog Bell Buoy rocked in the grey. 'My turn next,' whispered Milo, braver now. Pico flew ahead in a small circle and came back. 'Fog is thin, fog is kind, path is clear.' Keeper Noor carried Milo in the pocket of her coat — mice in pockets must hold very still, and Milo held still except for his whiskers. At the buoy, Milo rang the bell a second time — ding — and the buoy answered with its deep, slow note — dong — as if an older bell were saying thank you to a younger one. A seal lifted her head, listened, and slipped quietly away so she would not splash the sound. [Keeper's pause: child guesses — will the last bay be loud or quiet? Any guess is right; the keeper says, 'Let us go and see.']

BAY 3 — GULL ROCK. At the last bay, the beam rested on Gull Rock, where all the gulls sleep in a row, beaks tucked, even Pico's cousins. 'Quietest ring of all,' said Keeper Noor. Milo stood on the lowest, safest stone — Keeper Noor's hand stayed right beside him — and rang the bell a third and last time — ding, barely louder than a yawn. No boat answered. No buoy answered. Instead, every sleeping gull sighed at once, a soft harbour sigh that meant: all safe, all home, all heard. Pico landed, folded his wings, and said only, 'Home, home.' Biscuit, who had followed along the wall at a cat's own pace, arrived exactly in time for the ending, as cats do.

THE LAMP DIMS. Keeper Noor carried Milo back to his dry nook in the Harbour Wall, tucked the tiny bell beside him for tomorrow, and dimmed the lamp to its night glow. 'One light, three bays,' she said, 'one story safely home.' And the harbour slept.
