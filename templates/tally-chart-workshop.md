# The Tally & Chart Workshop

*Turn tallies into charts — and charts into answers*

**Category:** Education · **Ages:** Ages 7–10 · **Chinese name:** 计数与图表工坊 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A data-literacy studio where learners collect counts with tally marks, turn them into pictographs, bar graphs and line plots, then read the chart back to answer real questions and tell the data story in one clear sentence. Every set is fully worked: count in fives, choose a scale, label the axes, compare bars, and check the total. No speed rewards and no ranking labels — careful reading wins.

## Features

- Tally in fives, then build the chart step by step
- Pictographs, bar graphs and line plots with honest scales
- Compare, add and total questions with worked answers
- Data-story retell: one sentence that says what the chart proves
- Parent view: which chart types are steady and which need practice

## What's included

## Sample content

### Sample Lesson · The Garden Gate Tally — From Tallies to a Bar Graph (complete worked lesson)

THE QUESTION: Alex and the Garden Club watched the garden gate for one week and tallied every visitor. Which visitor came most often — and how do we prove it from the chart?

STEP 1 — COUNT THE TALLIES IN FIVES. Every fifth mark closes the gate (four uprights crossed by one diagonal), so a full gate is 5. Bees: two full gates + 2 = 12. Butterflies: one full gate + 4 = 9. Ladybugs: one full gate + 2 = 7. Sparrows: two full gates = 10. Check the total before drawing anything: 12 + 9 + 7 + 10 = 38 visitors in all.

STEP 2 — BUILD THE BAR GRAPH. Title: Garden Gate Visitors This Week. Horizontal axis labels, in tally order: Bees, Butterflies, Ladybugs, Sparrows. Vertical scale: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 — counting by twos, because the tallest bar (Bees, 12) must fit and odd counts like 9 and 7 land halfway between gridlines, which is fine: draw the bar to the exact height and label the number on top. Bars: Bees 12, Butterflies 9, Ladybugs 7, Sparrows 10.

STEP 3 — READ IT BACK (answers worked, not guessed). Q1 Which visitor came most often? Bees — the tallest bar, 12. Q2 Which came fewest? Ladybugs — the shortest bar, 7. Q3 How many more bees than ladybugs? 12 − 7 = 5 more bees. Q4 How many butterflies and sparrows together? 9 + 10 = 19. Q5 Does the chart match Step 1? Add the bar labels: 12 + 9 + 7 + 10 = 38 — the same total, so the graph is honest.

STEP 4 — PICTOGRAPH KEY. If one picture stands for 2 visitors, then Bees need 12 ÷ 2 = 6 pictures, Sparrows need 10 ÷ 2 = 5 pictures, Ladybugs need 7 ÷ 2 = 3 whole pictures and one half picture, and Butterflies need 9 ÷ 2 = 4 whole pictures and one half picture. A half picture is allowed only when the key says so — always read the key first.

DATA-STORY RETELL (the learner says it in one sentence): 'Bees visited our garden most often this week — 12 times, 5 more than ladybugs, the fewest visitors.'

TRY IT YOURSELF (answers at the end): The club tallies shell finds at the harbour: Spiral shells 8, Smooth shells 11, Striped shells 6. (a) What is the total? (b) Which shell was found most often? (c) How many more smooth than striped? ANSWERS: (a) 8 + 11 + 6 = 25. (b) Smooth shells, 11. (c) 11 − 6 = 5 more.
