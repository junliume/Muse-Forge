# Marble Run Architects

*Design the track, test gravity, land every marble home*

**Category:** Games · **Ages:** Ages 6–11 · **Chinese name:** 弹珠轨道建筑师 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A build-test-fix game about gravity in your hands. Players are marble-run architects: they place ramps, curves, chutes and splitters on a building board, predict where the marble will go and how fast, then let go and watch the test. When a run fails — and first runs are supposed to fail — the game teaches one small fix at a time: raise a wall, soften a slope, move the curve. Every level has a clear goal (ring the bell, land in the basket, miss the sand trap), a fixed set of pieces so the puzzle is fair, and a verified solution. Co-op mode lets two builders take turns placing one piece each and agreeing the prediction out loud before the drop. No timers, no speed rankings, no losing a life — a failed test is information, and the Fix-It Log celebrates what each test taught you.

## Features

- Build with a fixed, fair piece set — every level is solvable
- Predict before you drop: path, speed and one risk
- Test, watch, then fix ONE thing at a time
- Fix-It Log records what each test taught you
- Co-op mode: builders alternate placing pieces

## Sample content

### Sample · Level 1: The Three-Gate Run (complete build-and-test walkthrough)

LEVEL 1 — THE THREE-GATE RUN. Goal: the marble starts on the Start Shelf, must ring the Bell Gate, and must land in the Catch Basket. It must NOT land in the Sand Trap. Pieces you may use (exactly 7, no extras): 1 Start Shelf, 1 Long Ramp, 1 Wide Curve (turns left), 1 Drop Chute, 1 Y-Splitter, 1 Bell Gate, 1 Catch Basket. The Sand Trap is already printed on the board under the right arm of the Y-Splitter — it is a hazard, not a piece you place.

THE BOARD. Imagine a grid 4 columns wide (A–D, left to right) and 4 rows tall (1 at the top, 4 at the bottom). Height matters: a piece on row 1 is higher than a piece on row 2, so the marble speeds up as it falls and slows down on flat or curved pieces.

THE VERIFIED SOLUTION — place the pieces in this order:
Step 1. Start Shelf at A1 (top-left, height 3). The marble rests here. Prediction starts here: it can only roll right and down.
Step 2. Long Ramp from B1 down to B2. The ramp is steep enough to build speed but ends pointing DOWN, not sideways — that matters in Step 3.
Step 3. Wide Curve at B3, receiving the marble from above and turning it LEFT-to-RIGHT so it exits travelling right along row 3. Why a wide curve and not a sharp one: after the Long Ramp the marble is moving fast; a sharp curve at this speed would throw it over the low wall (this is the level's one planned risk — see Test 1).
Step 4. Bell Gate at C3. The marble passes through the gate on row 3 and rings the bell. Goal part 1 is now complete — and it is complete BEFORE any split, so no later choice can un-ring it.
Step 5. Y-Splitter at D3, entered from the left. Set its flipper to DOWN. The flipper is the whole puzzle: DOWN sends the marble into the Drop Chute below; RIGHT would send it toward the Sand Trap printed at the board's right edge.
Step 6. Drop Chute directly below the splitter at D4. The chute walls are high, so the marble drops straight and slow into the basket.
Step 7. Catch Basket at the foot of the Drop Chute. Goal part 2 completes when the marble lands and stays in the basket.

PREDICT (say it before you drop): Path — shelf, ramp, curve, bell, splitter, chute, basket. Speed — fast after the ramp, slower after the wide curve, slow and straight in the chute. One risk — overshooting the wide curve.

TEST 1 (the expected first failure): If the Wide Curve's outer wall is at its LOW setting, the fast marble hops the wall and lands off-board. That is a good test, not a loss. Fix ONE thing: raise the curve's outer wall to HIGH. Change nothing else, so you know the fix caused the result.
TEST 2 (verified pass): Shelf (release) → Long Ramp (speed builds) → Wide Curve, high wall (marble stays in, slows) → Bell Gate (RING — goal 1) → Y-Splitter, flipper DOWN → Drop Chute (straight, slow) → Catch Basket (marble lands and stays — goal 2). Sand Trap: untouched, because the flipper pointed down, not right. Level clear in 2 tests.

FIX-IT LOG (fill in after every test): Test number / What I changed / What happened / What it taught me. Example row from this level: 1 / Nothing yet — first build / Marble hopped the curve / Fast marbles need high walls on curves.
REFLECT (answer out loud): 1) Which piece slowed the marble down the most? (The Wide Curve.) 2) Which single setting decided between the basket and the trap? (The splitter flipper.) 3) If you removed the Drop Chute, what would go wrong? (The marble would fall sideways off row 3 instead of dropping straight into the basket.)

DESIGNER'S VERIFICATION NOTE: the path visits each of the 7 pieces exactly once, the bell is rung before the only choice point (the splitter), the trap is reachable only on the wrong flipper setting, and both flipper settings were checked — DOWN passes, RIGHT lands in the trap and prompts a re-test. There is no placement of the 7 pieces other than the one above that both rings the bell and reaches the basket, because the Bell Gate must sit on row 3 to the right of the curve's exit, and the basket must sit below the splitter. The puzzle is therefore fair, solvable, and has exactly one solution to find.
