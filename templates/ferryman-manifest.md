# The Ferryman's Manifest

*A one-item-at-a-time river-crossing logic game*

**Category:** Games · **Ages:** Ages 7 and up · **Chinese name:** 摆渡人的货单 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

An original river-crossing logic game. The ferryman's boat holds the ferryman plus exactly one item, and some pairs can never be left alone together on a bank. Players plan the full crossing order, test a move, and watch the banks update — a wrong pairing is spotted gently and can be undone, never punished. Every puzzle in the pack is solvable and verified end to end, and the reasoning (which pair is unsafe, and why the middle item must cross first) is explained in plain words after the solve.

## Features

- Verified solvable crossing puzzles — every pack checked end to end
- Plan, test and undo moves — no punishment for a wrong pairing
- Plain-words reasoning explained after each solve
- Three difficulties: 3, 4 and 5 items

## What's included

- Crossing puzzle pack (3 difficulties)
- Move planner with undo
- Reasoning walkthrough
- Gentle hint ladder

## At a glance

- One boat, one item at a time — plan who crosses, who waits, and who must never be left together
- Spot the unsafe pair first: the whole puzzle turns on which two items can never share a bank
- Undo any move and try a new order — the banks update live so the logic is always visible

## How it works

1. Read the manifest and spot the unsafe pair — the two items that can never be left alone on a bank
2. Plan the crossing order, load one item at a time, and watch both banks update after every move
3. Undo any move freely; after the solve, read the plain-words reasoning for why the order works

## Sample content

### Sample Puzzle · The Three-Item Crossing (complete verified solve)

THE SCENE: The river is wide, the current is kind, and the ferry is very, very small. Ferryman Pip rows a flat wooden boat that holds exactly two: Pip, and ONE item at a time — never two. Waiting on the near bank this morning are three passengers who cannot row themselves: Milo the cat, calm as a scone; a woven basket of fish, silver and still as spoons; and a stout sack of seeds, tied twice at the top. Everything must reach the far bank. Nothing may be eaten on the way. That last part is the whole puzzle.

THE DISCOVERY QUESTION (ask this before any move is made): Three items, two banks, one small boat. Some pairs of items are perfectly safe left alone together. One pair is not. Which pair can never be left alone on a bank — and why? Take your time and look at what each item is: a cat, a basket of fish, a sack of seeds. (Answer, kept for the planner: Milo + the fish basket is the one unsafe pair — left alone together, Milo eats the fish. Milo + seeds is a safe pair: cats do not eat seeds. Fish + seeds is a safe pair: neither one can eat anything at all.) Find the unsafe pair first; the rest of the puzzle is simply keeping that pair apart whenever the ferryman is not standing right there with them.

THE RULES, IN FULL: The boat holds the ferryman plus exactly one item. Items waiting on a bank are alone together unless the ferryman is standing on that same bank. A pair is only unsafe when it is left ALONE — if the ferryman is present, any pair is fine, because Pip simply holds Milo's collar and hums. And every move can be undone: rowing back is never a mistake, it is part of the plan.

A GENTLE WRONG MOVE (try it, watch what happens, undo it): Suppose you load the sack of seeds first and row it across. Now look back at the near bank: Milo and the fish basket are standing there alone together — the one unsafe pair. That is the trap of this puzzle in a single picture: the seeds are the easiest item to carry and the most dangerous one to carry first, because moving them first abandons the dangerous pair. No harm done — undo the move, row the seeds back, and start again. (Notice that taking the fish first does not spring the trap either: it leaves Milo with the seeds, a safe pair. But the plan below takes Milo first, because Milo is the item the unsafe pair is about.)

WHY MILO CROSSES FIRST: Milo is the middle item — the only item who belongs to the unsafe pair as the one who does the eating. The seeds are safe with everyone. The fish are unsafe with exactly one thing: Milo. So the whole game is about supervising a single relationship: Milo and the fish may share a bank only while the ferryman is physically standing there. Taking Milo across first parks the troublemaker alone on the far bank, where he can harm nothing, and lets the fish travel later to a bank the ferryman is standing on. Then Milo — and only Milo — is the one who must ride back, because the fish can wait alone safely, but Milo can never be left waiting with the fish.

THE VERIFIED SOLUTION — SEVEN CROSSINGS, BANK BY BANK:
Start — Near bank: Milo, fish, seeds, and the ferryman (safe, supervised). Far bank: empty.
Crossing 1 — Take Milo across. Near bank: fish + seeds (safe — neither can eat anything). Far bank: Milo, with the ferryman.
Crossing 2 — The ferryman returns alone. Near bank: fish + seeds, ferryman back. Far bank: Milo alone (safe — nothing there to eat or chase).
Crossing 3 — Take the fish across. Near bank: seeds alone (safe). Far bank: Milo + fish together — but the ferryman is standing right there, so the unsafe pair is supervised and safe.
Crossing 4 — Bring Milo back. Near bank: Milo + seeds (a safe pair). Far bank: fish alone (safe — fish cannot eat anything, not even each other).
Crossing 5 — Take the seeds across. Near bank: Milo alone (safe). Far bank: fish + seeds (the calmest pair in the puzzle — safe).
Crossing 6 — The ferryman returns alone. Near bank: Milo, with the ferryman back. Far bank: fish + seeds (safe).
Crossing 7 — Take Milo across. Near bank: empty. Far bank: Milo, fish, seeds — all three across, nothing eaten, puzzle solved.

THE REASONING RECAP (read aloud together after the solve): Every crossing in this puzzle was decided by one question — who is left alone with whom? The unsafe pair was Milo and the fish, and they were never once left alone together: they only ever shared a bank while the ferryman stood between them. Milo, the middle item, crossed first so the dangerous pair began the game on opposite banks. The fish crossed to a supervised bank. Milo rode back because he was the only passenger who could not be left with what was waiting on the far side. The seeds, safe with everyone, crossed when the plan needed them. Seven crossings, one rule, zero fish eaten — that is the whole logic, and it is yours now: find the unsafe pair, move the middle item first, and never leave the pair alone.
