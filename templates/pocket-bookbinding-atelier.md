# Pocket Bookbinding Atelier

*Bind a small notebook by hand, one quiet signature at a time*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Grown-Ups · **Chinese name:** 口袋装订工坊 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A calm, hands-on studio for grown-ups who want to make a real notebook instead of buying another one. Start with one folded signature, learn the three-hole pamphlet stitch, and finish a pocket notebook in a single sitting — then vary paper, cover and thread to make a small set for notes, lists and letters. Every step is measured and sequenced so a first-time binder succeeds, with gentle options for arthritic hands, low-vision makers, and anyone working at a kitchen table with basic tools.

## At a glance

- Fold one signature, punch three holes, stitch in-out-in — a real notebook in one sitting
- Choose paper for the job: sketch, fountain-pen, or everyday notes
- Press overnight, trim once, and open to a first page worth writing on

## How it works

1. Fold four sheets into one signature, wrap the cover around it, and crease every fold firmly
2. Measure the three holes — 25 mm, 74 mm, 123 mm — punch them, and stitch in, out, in, out
3. Knot, press overnight under a heavy book, and open to the first-page ritual

## Sample content

### Sample Session · Your First Pocket Notebook: One Signature, Three Holes (complete worked make)

ATELIER CARD — Maker: Alex · A6 pocket notebook · Everyday 80gsm paper · Plum card cover · Three-hole pamphlet stitch · About 60 min · Step-by-step guided. Today's make: one small notebook, bound by hand from four sheets of paper and a single length of thread — the atelier's classic first session.

WHAT YOU ARE MAKING
An A6 pocket notebook, 105 × 148 mm (about 4.1 × 5.8 in) — sixteen pages from four sheets. Small enough for a coat pocket, sturdy enough for daily lists, and honest work: every part of it was folded, punched and stitched by you.

MATERIALS (gather everything before you sit down)
• 4 sheets of A5 paper (148 × 210 mm). No A5 to hand? Take 2 sheets of US letter paper, cut each one in half across the short side to make four half-sheets (about 5.5 × 8.5 in), and use those exactly the same way — the finished notebook comes out a breath taller, and that is fine.
• 1 heavier cover sheet — card stock or stiff paper, the same size as your pages or a few millimetres larger all round.
• Linen thread or embroidery floss, about 45 cm (18 in) — roughly three times the height of the notebook, plus a hand's width to spare.
• A large-eye needle (a darning or bookbinding needle; the generous eye matters more than the point).
• An awl or a push-pin, for punching the holes.
• A bone folder, or the back of a spoon, for firm creases.
• A ruler, a pencil, and a piece of scrap cardboard to use as a punching mat.

STEP 1 — FOLD THE SIGNATURE (10 minutes)
Fold each of the four sheets in half, short edge to short edge. A note about grain: paper folds most willingly along its grain. If a fold feels springy and keeps trying to pop open, you are folding across the grain — that is normal for these sheets, and a firm crease settles it. Run the bone folder (or the spoon back) along each fold twice, working away from the centre, until the crease is sharp and the sheet lies flat and quiet. Then nest the four folded sheets inside one another, folds aligned — this little bundle is one signature. Wrap the cover sheet around the outside, fold and crease it the same way, then tap the whole bundle on the table, spine down, so every edge lines up.

STEP 2 — MARK AND PUNCH THREE HOLES (5 minutes)
Open the signature flat with the inside of the fold facing up, and lay the fold line against your ruler. The spine is 148 mm tall, and the three holes sit at 25 mm, 74 mm and 123 mm measured from the top edge: one hole 25 mm (about 1 in) down from the top, one exactly at mid-height (148 ÷ 2 = 74 mm), and one 25 mm up from the bottom edge (148 − 25 = 123 mm). Check the spacing before you punch: 25 → 74 is 49 mm, and 74 → 123 is 49 mm — even gaps, comfortably inside the page. Mark the three points lightly in pencil on the fold, close the signature, set it fold-down on the scrap cardboard, and push the awl (or push-pin) straight through all the layers at each mark. Give the awl a quarter-turn wiggle to open each hole just enough for the needle to pass without a fight.

STEP 3 — THE PAMPHLET STITCH (10 minutes)
Thread the needle with the full 45 cm and do not knot the end. Work slowly — the sequence is in, out, in, out, and it checks itself at the end.
1. Start OUTSIDE the spine: push the needle IN through the centre hole, and leave a tail of about 7 cm hanging outside the spine. Keep hold of that tail — it is half of your knot.
2. From inside the fold, push the needle OUT through the top hole. Pull the thread snug, not tight.
3. From outside, push the needle IN through the bottom hole, skipping the centre. The thread now runs down the outside of the spine.
4. From inside, push the needle OUT through the centre hole again. Both thread ends now meet outside the spine at the centre hole.
5. Tie the 7 cm tail and the working thread together in one firm knot, snugged down over the long stitch at the centre hole, and trim both ends to about 10 mm — a neat little tassel.
CHECK YOUR WORK: inside the centre fold there should be one long stitch running the full height, from the top hole down to the bottom hole. Outside the spine you should see three segments: the run from the top hole, the run to the bottom hole, and the knotted pair sitting at the middle. If your pattern matches, the binding is correct — it really is that simple, and that strong.

STEP 4 — FINISHING (5 minutes, plus overnight)
Close the notebook and slide it under a heavy book — a dictionary or a stack of cookbooks — and leave it to press overnight. Tomorrow it will open flat and feel like a bought thing, only better. If the page edges step out unevenly, you may trim just 2–3 mm off the fore-edge, or leave it softly uneven; both are honest finishes. Then keep the first-page ritual, as every atelier notebook does: on page one, write today's date, the notebook's job in three words ('Everyday notes', in Alex's first copy), and one line you want to remember. The notebook begins its life already useful.

TROUBLESHOOTING
• Loose stitch: never yank one end. Re-tension evenly, walking the slack from hole to hole with the needle tip until every segment is equally snug, then re-knot.
• Torn hole: glue a small paper patch — a 15 mm square of matching paper — over the tear on the inside of the fold, let it dry fully, and re-punch through the patch. The repair disappears into the fold.
• Crooked fold: fix it before stitching, never after. Open the signature, re-crease each sheet firmly with the bone folder, re-nest the sheets, and only then punch and stitch.
