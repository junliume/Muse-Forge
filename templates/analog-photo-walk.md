# The Analog Photo Walk

*Slow walks, 12 frames, and a contact sheet you keep*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Grown-ups · **Chinese name:** 胶片散步手记 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A slow photography walk for grown-ups, borrowed from film days without needing a film camera: you choose a route and a small frame budget — 6, 12 or 24 frames — and the walk gives you one gentle looking prompt per frame, so you look longer before you click. No deleting on the walk and no reviewing each shot; when you get home you lay the frames out in order as a contact sheet, pick one favourite, and write three honest lines about it. Any phone or camera works; the discipline, not the gear, is the point.

## Features

- A small frame budget that slows you down — look first, click once, walk on
- Looking prompts in plain language: light & shadow, hand-painted signs, a colour that repeats, everyday details noticed slowly
- No chimping rule, kindly kept: no deleting and no per-shot reviewing until you are home
- Contact-sheet journal lays frames out in walk order, so the walk reads like a short story
- Favourite-frame reflection: three honest lines — what you saw, why you stopped, what you would wait for next time

## Sample content

### Sample Walk · Old Town Lanes, Twelve Frames (complete first walk)

WALK CARD — Walker: Alex · Old town lanes · 12 frames · One prompt per frame · Light & shadow + Everyday details · About 30 min · Contact sheet + short notes · English. The two walk rules, said once at the door: (1) Look first, click once, walk on — no deleting and no reviewing frames until you are home. (2) Twelve frames is the whole budget; a frame you save is a frame you still have at the last corner.

BEFORE YOU LEAVE (3 minutes)
Put the camera or phone on its simplest setting and put it in your pocket, not in your hand. Read the first prompt now, then do not look at the screen again until the first corner. Carry a small notebook or open the journal page: after each frame you will write only the frame number and three words — no more. Those three words are your field notes; the looking itself is the walk.

FRAMES 1–4 — THE SLOW START
Frame 1 prompt: THE FIRST LIGHT YOU ACTUALLY NOTICE. Walk until light does something you would normally pass — striping a wall, pooling on a doorstep, catching the rim of a parked bicycle. Stop. Look for a full slow count of ten before you raise the camera. Frame it so the light, not the object, is the subject. Click once. Field note example: "1 — stripes, bakery wall."
Frame 2 prompt: A SIGN SOMEONE PAINTED BY HAND. Old town lanes keep them: a bakery board, a mended shop sign, a house number painted twice because the first try faded. Photograph it straight on, filling the frame, the way you would copy a line into a notebook. "2 — blue numbers, painted twice."
Frame 3 prompt: AN EVERYDAY DETAIL AT HAND HEIGHT. A latch polished bright by thumbs, a rope handle, a window box watered this morning. Get close enough that the detail fills the frame and the street disappears. "3 — bright latch, green door."
Frame 4 prompt: THE COLOUR THAT KEEPS REPEATING. By the fourth corner you will have seen it three times — today it was shutters, a scarf in a window, and a delivery crate, all the same dusty teal. Wait for the fourth instance. That wait is the exercise. "4 — teal again, crate."

FRAMES 5–8 — THE MIDDLE, WHERE ATTENTION DIPS
Frame 5 prompt: A THRESHOLD. A doorway, a gate, the exact line where lane becomes courtyard. Stand to one side so the frame holds both sides of the line — outside and in — in one picture. "5 — gate line, sun/shade."
Frame 6 prompt: WORK, FROM A RESPECTFUL DISTANCE. Someone sweeping, unloading, repairing — photographed wide, from across the lane, never close and never posed. If anyone minds, smile, lower the camera, and spend the frame on their tools instead: a broom, a ladder, a row of tins. People are neighbours here, not subjects. "6 — sweeping, wide, kind."
Frame 7 prompt: LOOK UP. Balconies, washing lines, a chimney against fast cloud. Old towns stack their lives vertically; most walks only photograph the ground floor. "7 — lines and laundry, sky."
Frame 8 prompt: THE FRAME YOU ALMOST DID NOT TAKE. By now the budget feels small and every click feels expensive — that feeling is the film lesson working. Take the ordinary corner you were about to save your frames past: bins, a bench, a plain wall with one crack shaped like a river. Ordinary frames are the ones that read truest in a month. "8 — plain wall, river crack."

FRAMES 9–12 — SPEND THE LAST FRAMES SLOWLY
Frame 9 prompt: A REFLECTION THAT IS NOT A MIRROR. A shop window holding the lane behind you, a puddle holding a roof. Frame it so a viewer has to look twice to know which way is real. "9 — window holds the lane."
Frame 10 prompt: SOUND, PHOTOGRAPHED. You cannot hear a picture, but you can photograph what makes a sound: the fountain, the loose shutter in the breeze, the cafe cups. Choose the sound you have been hearing longest on this walk. "10 — fountain, all along."
Frame 11 prompt: SAVE NOTHING — THE SECOND-TO-LAST FRAME. Photograph the view back the way you came. Walks look different in reverse; one frame facing home is how the contact sheet learns to tell a story with an ending. "11 — looking back, long lane."
Frame 12 prompt: THE LAST FRAME IS A GIFT FRAME. Give it to the smallest good thing still unphotographed — a sparrow on a sill, chalk on a step, the first lamp lit. Do not save it for something better further on; there is no further on. That is the point of a budget. "12 — lamp lit, sparrow gone."

BACK HOME — THE CONTACT SHEET (10 minutes)
Lay the twelve frames out in walk order, 1 to 12, without reordering, cropping or deleting — the sheet is a record of attention, not a gallery. Under each frame copy your three-word field note. Then read the sheet once, top to bottom, like a twelve-line poem: stripes, blue numbers, bright latch, teal again, gate line, sweeping, laundry sky, river crack, window lane, fountain, looking back, lamp lit. Notice what the walk was actually about — today, thresholds and teal — which you could not have known at frame 1.

THE FAVOURITE FRAME — THREE HONEST LINES
Pick ONE favourite (today: frame 5, the gate line) and write exactly three lines. Line 1, WHAT YOU SAW: "Half the courtyard in sun, half in shade, with the gate standing exactly on the border." Line 2, WHY YOU STOPPED: "Because I cross lines like that all day and never once look at them." Line 3, WHAT YOU WOULD WAIT FOR NEXT TIME: "A person crossing it — I would wait five full minutes for the frame to complete itself." Three lines, no more. Close the journal. The next walk starts with an empty budget and the same two rules: look first, click once, walk on.
