# The Listening Room

*Music evenings that turn listening into conversation*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Adults · **Chinese name:** 聆听小屋 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A listening-evening studio for grown-ups: pick an era, a mood and a gathering size, and each evening unfolds as three guided listening blocks with story prompts between tracks. No expertise needed — the host script, pause points and conversation cards are all written for you, so friends, partners or a solo listener can slow down, listen closely, and trade the memories and images the music brings up. Evenings end with a one-line keepsake note, never a score or ranking.

## Features

- Three listening blocks with written host script and pause points
- Conversation cards that link sound to memory and story

## Sample content

### Sample · Evening One: Rain, Rivers & Return (complete hosted evening)

EVENING CARD — Host: Alex · Small circle (3–5) · Warm & nostalgic · About 60 min · Guided listening + talk. Tonight's music is described, never named: choose any recordings that match each block — a gentle solo-piano nocturne-style piece, a flowing folk ballad with strings, and a warm jazz-trio closer.

HOST OPENING (about 4 min) — Alex says: "Welcome to the Listening Room. Tonight is simple: we listen first, and we talk after. Three pieces of music, three short conversations, and one sentence to take home. Phones away now — face down, or in another room; the music gets our whole attention. While a piece plays, nobody talks, not even me. When it ends, I will let the silence sit for one breath before I read the card. There are no right answers and no music knowledge needed: if a piece reminds you of a place, a person, or a colour, that is exactly the kind of thing we trade here. One voice at a time, and a pass is always a complete answer."

BLOCK 1 — RAIN (listening: about 6–8 min). The piece: a gentle solo-piano nocturne in a slow, rocking tempo — single high notes falling like rain against a window, a soft rolling pattern underneath, the melody returning twice and a little different each time, as if the weather itself were changing its mind. Let it play all the way to the end; do not fade it. PAUSE POINT: when the last note fades, count five slow breaths of silence before anyone speaks. CONVERSATION CARD: "What place did the rain in this music sound like?" Follow-up prompts: (1) "Was it raining hard or gently in your place — and how could you tell from the music alone?" (2) "Who else was in that place, even if they never quite appeared?" (3) "If the rain in this piece could wash one thing clean, what would it be?" Host note: go around the circle once. Short answers are fine — one person's image will remind the next person of theirs.

BLOCK 2 — RIVERS (listening: about 7 min). The piece: a flowing folk ballad carried by fingerpicked guitar and warm strings — a melody that keeps moving downstream, verse to verse, never fully stopping, with a wordless hum joining near the end like a second traveller falling into step alongside. PAUSE POINT: the end of the final hum; let the room hear its own quiet for a moment before the card. CONVERSATION CARD: "A journey this river sound suggests — who is travelling, and what are they carrying?" Follow-up prompts: (1) "Is the traveller in a hurry, or in no hurry at all — what in the music tells you?" (2) "What do they set down, or pick up, somewhere along the way?" (3) "Where does the river finally let them rest?" Host note: this is the storytelling block. If the circle is enjoying it, let one answer grow long; depth matters more than covering every prompt.

BLOCK 3 — RETURN (listening: about 6 min). The piece: a warm jazz trio — brushed drums soft as footsteps on a familiar street, a walking double bass, and a piano that states a simple home-theme melody, wanders away from it, and finds its way back twice. PAUSE POINT: the final return of the theme. When the room realises it is the home theme again, smile, and let the piece finish entirely in silence. CONVERSATION CARD: "What does coming home sound like in this piece — an instrument, a rhythm, a silence?" Follow-up prompts: (1) "Which moment felt most like arriving: the first notes, the walking bass, or the quiet at the very end?" (2) "What is one sound from a real home you have lived in that you can still hear today?" (3) "If your own coming-home had a theme, would it be loud or quiet — and played on what?"

CLOSING RITUAL (about 5 min) — One sentence each, going around the circle once: "Tonight, the sound I will keep is…" No explanations needed; the sentence is the whole gift. Alex gathers the sentences into the evening's keepsake note and reads it aloud to close. Worked example — tonight's keepsake note: "Evening One: the rain sounded like the window of a house we once lived in, the river carried us further than we planned, and coming home sounded like a piano finding its way back — slowly, and sure of the door." Alex closes: "Thank you for listening the way you did. That is the whole evening: three pieces, three conversations, one line to keep."

HOST TIPS — Volume: quieter than you first think; if guests lean in slightly, it is right. Seating: a semicircle facing the speaker or record player, so everyone shares the same sound and can see each other for the cards. One voice at a time during the conversations — the talk is part of the music. Keep water or tea within reach so nobody has to leave mid-block. And never fade a piece early to save time: the ending is where the evening's silence comes from.
