# The Balance Beam Bakery

*A cooperative balance-scale puzzle game — weigh, swap, and make both sides equal*

**Category:** Games · **Ages:** Ages 6–11 · **Chinese name:** 天平面包房 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A cooperative puzzle game played on a bakery balance scale. Each level deals number tokens — loaves, berries and shells carved with numbers — onto the scale's two pans, and players add, move, swap or deduce until both pans total exactly the same. Three puzzle types rotate through every pack: Balancing Totals (build two equal sets from the dealt tokens), One-Move Fixes (the scale starts tilted, and a single swap rebalances it — and that swap is unique), and Mystery Weights (deduce a wrapped token's weight from two balance clues, then use it in a final balance). There are no timers and no losing: a tilted scale just wobbles, waits, and invites another try. A three-step hint ladder — nudge, clue, worked step — keeps every level solvable without ever giving the answer first, and totals scale from 20 to 100 with the number-range setting. What's inside: a level card pack of 30 balance puzzles in the 3 types, a printable balance mat and token set, hint ladder cards, and mystery-weight challenge envelopes. How it works: pick a level card, set the tokens on the mat, predict before you peek, then weigh, swap and deduce together until the beam sits level.

## Sample content

### Sample Level · Level 1: The Festival Scale (complete worked level, all three rounds verified)

LEVEL CARD — Player: the scale-keeper · Ages 6–11 · About 20 min · Place: the festival bakery stall · The loop on every round: Predict → Weigh → Adjust → Check.

SETUP — THE FESTIVAL SCALE
The festival scale has two pans. Tokens are carved with numbers. Lay the printable balance mat on the table, set the brass scale in the middle, and keep the hint ladder cards face down — nudge on top, then clue, then worked step. This level plays all three puzzle types, one round each.

ROUND 1 — BALANCING TOTALS
The tokens: 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 11. First add them all: 2 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 8 + 11 = 36. The total is 36, so each pan must reach exactly half of it: 36 ÷ 2 = 18. One rule: the 7-token must be used.
Worked solution: Left pan 7 + 11 = 18. Right pan 2 + 3 + 5 + 8 = 18 — check it step by step: 2 + 3 = 5, 5 + 5 = 10, 10 + 8 = 18. Balanced: 18 = 18. ✓
Note there is exactly one other solution: 7 + 8 + 3 = 18 against 2 + 5 + 11 = 18 — either wins the level.

ROUND 2 — ONE-MOVE FIX
The scale starts tilted: Left pan 11 + 5 + 3 = 19, Right pan 8 + 7 + 2 = 17. The left side is heavier by 2 (19 − 17 = 2). You may swap exactly one token from each pan — no more, no less.
Think first: swapping one token from each pan changes both sides. To fix a gap of 2, the swapped pair must differ by exactly 1, because each side moves by the difference between the two swapped tokens — the heavy side drops by that difference and the light side rises by it, closing the gap by twice 1 = 2. Now hunt for a pair that differs by exactly 1. The only pair that differs by 1 is the 3 (left) and the 2 (right).
Swap them: Left 11 + 5 + 2 = 18, Right 8 + 7 + 3 = 18. Balanced in exactly one move — and no other single swap works. That is what makes it a One-Move Fix. ✓

ROUND 3 — MYSTERY WEIGHT
A wrapped token W has no number. Two clue scales will tell you its weight.
Clue scale 1: W + 5 balances 11, so W = 11 − 5 = 6.
Clue scale 2 (check): W + W balances 5 + 7 = 12, and 6 + 6 = 12 — consistent, so W = 6 is confirmed.
Final balance using W: Left pan W + 11 = 6 + 11 = 17; Right pan 8 + 7 + 2 = 17. Balanced: 17 = 17. ✓

CLOSING — INVENT YOUR OWN TILT
The level card ends by asking the player to invent a tilt of their own with a gap of 2 and challenge a partner to find the unique fixing swap. Remember the keeper's rule from Round 2: for a gap of 2, the fixing pair must differ by exactly 1 — hide your pair well, and check your partner's answer by weighing it, never by peeking.

BAKER'S NOTE: There are no timers and no losing at this stall. A tilted scale just wobbles and waits. If a round sticks, climb the hint ladder one rung at a time — nudge, then clue, then worked step — and the level stays yours to solve.
