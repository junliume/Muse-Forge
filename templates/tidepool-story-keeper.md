# The Tidepool Story Keeper

*Tiny sea stories that grow one gentle chapter at a time*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 5–9 · **Chinese name:** 潮池故事守护人 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A gentle chapter-story keeper for the shore: each session opens a clear tidepool, meets its small residents, and follows one kind, unhurried chapter where the listener helps without taking and leaves every home as it was found. Chapters are short, read slowly, and end with a quiet closing so the story can continue tomorrow exactly where it paused. No collecting, no scores, no rushing — just looking closely, listening, and caring for a place by leaving it whole.

## Features

- One complete gentle chapter per session, read slowly
- Meet the tidepool residents — hermit crab, sea star, shrimp and gull
- Helping-without-taking theme in every chapter
- Quiet wind-down closing, resumable tomorrow
- Parent chapter log with theme and new words

## Sample content

### Sample · Chapter 1: The Shell That Hummed (complete chapter)

CHAPTER 1 — THE SHELL THAT HUMMED

At low tide, the cove kept its secrets in shallow pools. Alex knelt beside the clearest one and did not dip a finger in. “We look first,” Alex whispered. “We let them stay home.”

Inside the pool, a sea star rested orange against the rock, and a hermit crab named Tuck backed into a speckled shell, peeked out, and backed in again.

Then Alex heard it — a tiny hum, soft as a kettle far away. It came from an empty shell at the pool’s edge, pink inside and smooth as a spoon.

“Do shells hum?” Alex asked.

Tuck clicked one claw. “Only when they are remembering the sea,” he said, “and only if someone listens without taking.”

So Alex listened. The hum grew into three slow notes, then stopped. In the quiet, the sea star stretched one arm toward a trapped ribbon of seaweed where a baby shrimp, no bigger than a comma, turned in worried circles.

“We can help without grabbing,” Alex said. With a flat leaf, Alex gently lifted the seaweed’s edge. The water slipped free. The shrimp darted out, paused — as if to say thank you — and vanished under the rock.

Tuck chose a new shell that afternoon, leaving his old one clean and empty for someone smaller. Alex placed it by the pool, door facing the tide.

When the water returned, it would carry the hum onward, and the cove would remember that Alex had helped, and taken nothing at all.
