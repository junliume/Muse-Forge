# The Clock & Compass Workshop

*Hands-on time, calendars and directions — learned by doing*

**Category:** Education · **Ages:** Ages 6–9 · **Chinese name:** 时钟与指南针工坊 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A hands-on workshop where children learn to read analog and digital clocks, work out elapsed time, read calendars and simple timetables, and use compass directions on a room-and-map scale. Every lesson starts with a real object to move or point to, teaches one skill with a fully worked example, then gives short practice with answers explained — never speed-timed, never ranked. Mistakes get a calm re-teach with a different example.

## Sample content

### Sample Lesson · Maya and the Library Bus (telling time, elapsed time & timetables — fully worked)

LESSON CARD — Learner: Maya · Ages 7–8 · English · About 15 min · Focus: telling time to 5 minutes + elapsed time · Tools on the table: one moveable clock, one paper compass rose, one calendar page, one bus timetable card.

WARM-UP — READ THE CLOCK (three times, hands first)

Every clock tells its time with two hands. The short hand is the hour hand — it crawls. The long hand is the minute hand — it strides all the way around the face. Always read the hour first, then the minutes.

1) 9:00. The short hour hand points straight at the 9 and the long minute hand points straight up at the 12. Straight up means zero minutes past the hour, so this is nine o'clock exactly.

2) 9:15 — quarter past nine. The minute hand has walked a quarter of the way around the face and points at the 3 — and each number on the clock is worth 5 minutes, so 3 × 5 = 15 minutes past. The hour hand has crept just a little way past the 9, because a quarter of its journey to 10 is done. When the minute hand points at the 3, we say quarter past.

3) 9:40. The minute hand points at the 8, and 8 × 5 = 40 minutes past the hour. The hour hand is now much closer to the 10 than to the 9 — it is travelling toward 10 but has not arrived, so the hour is still 9. Nine forty.

WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — ELAPSED TIME: THE LIBRARY BUS

The library bus leaves at 9:40 and arrives at 11:15. How long is the ride? Never count minute by minute — hop along an open number line in friendly jumps.

Hop 1: 9:40 → 10:00. From forty minutes past to the next whole hour is 20 minutes.
Hop 2: 10:00 → 11:00. One whole hour is 60 minutes.
Hop 3: 11:00 → 11:15. One last short hop of 15 minutes.

Add the hops: 20 + 60 + 15 = 95 minutes, and 95 minutes is 1 hour 35 minutes. Check it backwards: 11:15 take away 15 minutes is 11:00, take away 60 minutes is 10:00, take away 20 minutes is 9:40 — exactly where the bus started. The ride takes 1 hour 35 minutes.

WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — THE CALENDAR: COUNTING TEN DAYS

Today is Wednesday, November 4. Library day is in 10 days. What day of the week is library day, and what is the date?

Count in two jumps on the calendar page. Jump one: 7 days after a Wednesday is another Wednesday, and November 4 + 7 days = November 11, so Wednesday + 7 days = Wednesday, November 11. Jump two: 3 more days — Thursday November 12 (1), Friday November 13 (2), Saturday November 14 (3). So library day is Saturday, November 14. Maya draws a star on the calendar page.

WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — THE COMPASS: ONE QUARTER TURN

Lay the paper compass rose flat with N (north) pointing to the top of the page. Stand up and face north — really face the way the N points. Now turn right, one quarter turn, exactly like the minute hand moving from 12 to 3. Facing north and turning right points you EAST. North, east, south, west — the compass points run clockwise, the same way the clock hands travel.

Now read the room like a map. In Maya's room the door is on the north wall, the big window is on the east wall, the rug lies on the south side, and the bookshelf stands against the west wall. Stand in the middle and point: north — the door. Turn right, east — the window. Turn right again, south — the rug. Once more, west — the shelf. The whole room is a compass you can walk inside.

PRACTICE — FIVE QUESTIONS (every answer worked in full)

Q1. A story starts at 2:30 and ends at 3:05. How long is the story? Hop on the number line: 2:30 → 3:00 is 30 minutes; 3:00 → 3:05 is 5 minutes; 30 + 5 = 35 minutes. The story lasts 35 minutes.

Q2. What time is half past 4? Half past means the minute hand has gone halfway around and points straight down at the 6 — and 6 × 5 = 30 minutes. Half past 4 is 4:30, with the hour hand sitting halfway between the 4 and the 5.

Q3. Today is Friday. What day of the week is it in 5 days? Count forward on the calendar: Saturday (1), Sunday (2), Monday (3), Tuesday (4), Wednesday (5). Five days after Friday is Wednesday.

Q4. The bus timetable card reads 8:10, 8:30, 8:50. Maya reaches the stop at 8:22. Which bus does she catch, and how long does she wait? The 8:10 bus has already gone, so the next bus after 8:22 is the 8:30 bus. The wait is 8:30 − 8:22 = 8 minutes.

Q5. Maya faces south, toward the rug. She turns LEFT. Which way does she face now? Turn the compass rose with her and check: stand facing south and your left hand points east — so facing south, a left turn points EAST. (A right turn from south would point west; right turns always run clockwise, left turns run the other way.)

CLOSING RECAP

Today Maya read three clocks by their hands — hour hand first, minute hand second, five minutes for every number. She measured the library bus ride at 1 hour 35 minutes by hopping 20 + 60 + 15 along a number line, counted 10 days forward from Wednesday, November 4 to Saturday, November 14, and turned her own room into a compass: door north, window east, rug south, shelf west. Nothing was speed-timed and nothing was scored. Tomorrow the workshop opens the calendar page again and plans a whole week of mornings. Parent summary: telling time to 5 minutes — secure; elapsed time with number-line hops — secure; compass quarter turns from north and south — building nicely.
