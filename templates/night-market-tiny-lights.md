# The Night Market of Tiny Lights

*Wander lantern-lit stalls and co-create a tale at each one*

**Category:** Storytelling · **Ages:** Ages 4–9 · **Chinese name:** 小灯夜市故事集 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A gentle wandering story that only opens after dark. Your hero follows a string of tiny lights into a friendly night market where every stall offers not something to buy, but a piece of a story to co-create: a wish written on a lantern, a sound caught in a jar, a kindness traded for a song. At each stall Muse offers one slow line or one kind choice, your storyteller answers in their own words, and Muse repeats it back before the wander continues. There are no prices, no winners and no scary stalls — just five cosy stops, one small mystery about why the market's biggest lantern went dark, and a closing bell that gathers every piece your storyteller invented into a finished tale they led from start to end.

## Features

- Five-stall wandering story — one co-created piece at each stop
- Slow, patient pacing: one line, one choice, then confirm it back
- A kind little mystery: relight the market's biggest lantern together
- Voice-first: press and hold to speak, no typing needed
- Parent story log keeps the finished tale exactly as told

## How it works

1. Follow the string of tiny lights into the market and stop at the first stall
2. At each stall answer one slow question or make one kind choice in your own words — Muse repeats it back before you wander on
3. Gather the wish, the sound and the kindness at the closing bell and hear your finished tale read back under the stars

## Sample content

### Sample · First Wander: Alex and the Stall That Sold Sounds (complete sample session)

THE MARKET OPENS (opening, read slowly): The sun had just tucked itself behind the rooftops when Alex saw the lights. Not street lights. Smaller, warmer — a string of tiny paper lanterns swaying above an alley Alex had walked past a hundred times and never once noticed. A small moth, no bigger than a thumbprint, looped down from the first lantern. 'I am Momo,' said the moth. 'I keep the lights company. Tonight the big lantern at the top of the market has gone dark, and nobody knows why. Will you wander with me and ask the stalls?' Alex nodded, and the alley opened like a yawn of warm air and cinnamon. STALL ONE — THE LANTERN-WISH STALL: The keeper was folding paper lanterns that folded themselves back open, just to be helpful. 'Every lantern here carries one wish somebody whispered,' she said. 'For the big lantern to wake, it needs a brand-new wish. Not a big one. A true one.' MUSE ASKS, ONE QUESTION ONLY: What small, true wish should Alex whisper into the paper? (The storyteller answers in their own words. Muse repeats the wish back exactly, writes it on the lantern in glowing letters, and only then walks on.) STALL TWO — THE STALL THAT SOLD SOUNDS: This stall sold no things at all. Its shelves held glass jars, and inside each jar a sound slept curled like a cat: rain on a tin roof, a kettle beginning to sing, a frog clearing its throat. 'The big lantern is not broken,' whispered the keeper, opening one jar a crack so a sound like distant bells slipped out. 'It is listening. It went dark to hear better. It is waiting for a sound it has never heard before.' MUSE OFFERS ONE CHOICE: Which jar should Alex open — rain, kettle, frog, or a jar with no label at all? (Whichever jar is chosen, Muse describes its sound in one slow, funny sentence, the moth dances once in a circle, and the sound is carried carefully in cupped hands. There is no wrong jar.) STALL THREE — THE KIND-BARGAIN STALL: At the third stall nothing cost money. A sign read: TRADE A KINDNESS, GET A SONG. The keeper asked Alex to remember one kind thing — any kind thing, big as a umbrella shared or small as a worm moved off a path. When Alex told it, the whole stall sang one soft note, and from under the counter the keeper brought out the big lantern's lost matches — which were not matches at all, but three fireflies in a matchbox, fast asleep. THE CLOSING BELL (ending, gathered from the storyteller's own pieces): On the rooftop, Alex set the storyteller's wish-lantern down gently, opened cupped hands so the chosen sound could float up, and woke the fireflies by telling them the kindness out loud. One, two, three lights. The big lantern did not blaze — it glowed, the way a window glows when someone you love is home. Below, every stall keeper waved. Momo the moth settled on Alex's shoulder, very pleased with herself. 'Same time tomorrow?' she asked. 'The market has four more stalls,' she said, 'and every one of them is waiting for a story only you can tell.' THE END — the parent story log saves the wish, the chosen sound and the kindness exactly as the storyteller said them.
