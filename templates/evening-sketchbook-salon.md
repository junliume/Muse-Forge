# The Evening Sketchbook Salon

*A calm 20-minute drawing ritual for grown-ups — no talent required*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Grown-ups · **Chinese name:** 晚间速写沙龙 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A gentle evening drawing salon for grown-ups who think they cannot draw. Each session picks one ordinary subject — a mug, a window, a houseplant — and walks you through looking first, drawing second, in timed unhurried steps. There is no grading, no comparison gallery and no streak pressure: the point is twenty quiet minutes of actually seeing the thing in front of you. A 7-evening starter sequence builds from single objects to a small scene, and every session ends with a short see-don't-judge reflection.

## How it works

1. Settle in with one ordinary subject — a mug, a window, a houseplant
2. Look first for two full minutes before the pencil moves
3. Draw the contour slowly, then shade only the two biggest shadow shapes
4. Add one missed detail on a second pass, then close with two 'I noticed…' sentences

## Sample content

### Sample · Evening One: The Mug on the Table (complete 20-minute session)

EVENING ONE — THE MUG ON THE TABLE (20 minutes, pencil). Put any plain mug on a table where evening light falls across it. Sit down, feet flat, sketchbook open. MINUTES 0–2 — ONLY LOOK. Pencil down. Notice: where is the brightest patch on the mug? Where is the darkest shadow, and is its edge hard or soft? Follow the rim with your eyes — it is an oval, not a circle, from where you sit. MINUTES 2–7 — CONTOUR, SLOW. Draw the mug's outline in one unhurried line, eyes mostly on the mug, not the page. Wobbly is expected and fine. Add the handle as a simple C-curve. MINUTES 7–14 — SHADOW SHAPES. Squint until the scene turns into light and dark patches. Shade only the two biggest dark patches — the shadow inside the mug and the shadow it casts on the table — with soft side-of-pencil strokes. Leave the brightest patch as bare paper. MINUTES 14–18 — SECOND PASS. Look again for one thing you missed (most people find the small bright line where the rim catches light). Add just that one thing, nothing more. MINUTES 18–20 — REFLECTION, SEE-DON'T-JUDGE. Write two sentences under the drawing, starting with 'I noticed…': for example, 'I noticed the handle's shadow is longer than the handle. I noticed the rim oval gets thinner when I sit lower.' Never write whether the drawing is good. Close the book. Tomorrow, Evening Two: the same mug, from the other side.
