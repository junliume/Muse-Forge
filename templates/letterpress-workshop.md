# The Letterpress Workshop

*Set type by hand, ink it, and pull your first print*

**Category:** Grown-ups · **Ages:** Grown-ups · **Chinese name:** 活版印刷工坊 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A calm, hands-on studio for grown-ups who want to make something real with their hands. The Letterpress Workshop walks you through designing and printing a small letterpress card from start to finish — choosing a short phrase worth pressing into paper, setting metal-style type letter by letter in a composing stick, mixing ink to the colour you imagined, proofing the forme, and pulling a small edition you can keep or give away. Every step is explained in plain language with no prior experience assumed: what a chase is, why type reads backwards, how much ink is enough (less than you think), and how to read a proof like a printer. Sessions are unhurried and forgiving — a faint letter or a crooked line is not a failure, it is information, and the workshop shows you exactly how to fix it. Whether your project is a motto card for your own desk, an invitation, a bookplate or a poster phrase, you end each session with printed cards drying on the rack and a short care checklist that keeps the press ready for next time.

## Sample content

### Sample · Session 1: A Three-Line Motto Card (complete first session)

WORKSHOP CARD — Maker: Alex · Motto card · Warm cream paper · Plum + Burgundy ink · Making & craft · About 20 min · Step-by-step guided. Today's project: one small card, three lines, printed by hand — the workshop's classic first session.

STEP 1 — CHOOSE THE PHRASE (3 minutes)
A motto card holds about three short lines; more than that and the type case runs dry. Today's motto, written for this workshop: line one, MAKE IT BY HAND. Line two, ONE TRUE LINE AT A TIME. Line three, small and centred like a signature: — THE WORKSHOP —. Count before you set, because every letter is a physical piece of type. Line one is 15 letters and 3 spaces (18 pieces). Line two is 18 letters and 5 spaces (23 pieces). Line three is 11 letters, 2 spaces and 2 dashes (15 pieces). Total: 56 pieces of type — a comfortable first forme, and the case holds plenty of every letter except E, so check your E's first: this motto needs 7.

STEP 2 — SET THE TYPE (7 minutes)
Work in the composing stick, left to right, and remember the printer's oldest surprise: type sets upside down and reads right to left, so the finished card reads correctly. Set line two first — it is the longest line, so it decides the width of everything else. Slide each sort in snugly, letters nick-side out, and put a thin space between words, never two. Then line one above it and line three below, centred by adding equal spaces at both ends. Fill the gaps at the ends of shorter lines with blank spacing material so nothing can wobble. Read the whole forme backwards in the stick — printers read mirror text fluently within a week, and your first skill is simply checking that no letter is upside down: an inverted sort prints as a blank or a smudge.

STEP 3 — LOCK THE CHASE (2 minutes)
Slide the three lines out of the stick into the chase — the iron frame that holds the forme on the press bed. Surround the type with furniture (wood blocks) and two quoins, the little expanding wedges. Tighten the quoins a quarter-turn at a time, alternating, then lift the chase by one corner: a locked forme holds together as one piece and rings faintly, like a dull bell, when tapped. If anything shifts, loosen, nudge, re-lock. Never print an unlocked forme — type that moves mid-print is how sorts get crushed.

STEP 4 — MIX THE INK (3 minutes)
Today's colour is the workshop plum. On the ink disc, knife out roughly three parts burgundy to one part charcoal — the charcoal takes the sweetness off and gives the plum its evening depth. Roll the brayer through it fifty slow passes until the ink sounds like soft tape being pulled and looks like satin, not jam. Too much ink is the beginner's classic error: you want a film so thin the disc still shows through at the edges. Roll the inked brayer across the type twice, lightly — the letters should glisten, not drown.

STEP 5 — PULL AND READ A PROOF (3 minutes)
Lay one warm cream card on the tympan, lower it, and pull the impression. Peel the card up slowly from one corner and read the proof like a printer, top to bottom, letter by letter. Today's proof shows exactly the two faults this session teaches you to fix. FAULT ONE — a faint letter: the H in HAND is pale at its top edge. Cause: that one sort sits a hair lower than its neighbours, so it kisses the paper instead of pressing it. Fix: unlock just that corner, slip a single sheet of tissue — printers call it underlay — beneath the H, re-lock. FAULT TWO — an uneven edge: the right ends of the lines drift, dark and heavy at the bottom corner. Cause: the chase is tighter at one quoin than the other, so pressure leans. Fix: ease the tight quoin back a quarter-turn, tap the forme level with the planing block, re-lock both quoins evenly. Pull a second proof: the H stands full and plum-dark, both edges print even. That second proof goes in the workshop notebook, faults and fixes written beside it in pencil.

STEP 6 — PRINT THE EDITION (2 minutes)
An edition of 8 cards. Same rhythm every time: ink the forme lightly every second card, lay, lower, pull, peel, and rack each card face-up to dry, edges aligned like a small deck. Count out loud as you rack them — printers number editions, and eight is a kind number: two to keep, six to give. Let the cards dry a full hour before stacking; letterpress ink dries by sinking in, not by evaporating, so patience is part of the craft.

CLEANUP & CARE (closing checklist)
Scrape the leftover ink back into its tin — a session this size uses less than a teaspoon. Wipe the disc and brayer with a lint-free rag until the rag comes away nearly clean, then one drop of press oil on the brayer's axle. Unlock the chase, return every sort to its compartment in the case — E's back with E's, because tomorrow's phrase will need them too — and brush the forme area free of paper dust. Note in the workshop log: motto card, plum (3 burgundy : 1 charcoal), edition of 8, proof faults fixed: one underlaid H, one eased quoin. Next session suggestion from the workshop: the same motto, one size larger, on soft kraft — and compare how the plum deepens on brown paper.
