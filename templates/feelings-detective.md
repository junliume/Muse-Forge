# Classroom Feelings Detective

*Read the clues, guess what a classmate is feeling*

**Category:** Games · **Also listed under:** Education · **Ages:** Ages 5–8 · **Chinese name:** 课堂感受侦探 · **Spec version:** 1.1

## What it is

An interactive detective game: each scene offers clues from expressions, body language, and setting, and kids reason step by step about what a classmate is feeling, why, and what they could do. It builds observation, empathy, and feelings vocabulary — wrong guesses are never punished; more clues simply arrive.

## Features

- Clue-based reasoning: expression + action + setting, always in that order
- Feelings word cards: happy, left out, nervous, angry, proud, embarrassed, left-out-but-hopeful and other blended feelings
- Two-feelings-at-once scenes in later packs: a classmate can be excited AND nervous, and detectives learn to name both
- Wrong guesses add clues — no punishment, no labels, no case ever closes unsolved
- Say-it-back step: before choosing, the detective repeats the clues in their own words, so reasoning is spoken, not just tapped
- Each scene ends with: what would you do? — plus a gentler follow-up, what would make tomorrow easier?
- Kind-action rehearsal: the chosen action is acted out in one short line of dialogue, so the child practises the words, not just the idea
- Parents can see practice history, including which feelings words their child has collected and used

## Sample content

### Case · The Empty Chair at Lunch (complete scene)

THE SCENE — THE EMPTY CHAIR (as played): It is lunchtime. Theo carries his tray with both hands and stops at the end of the table where he always sits. His chair is there. But today two new kids are sitting in the seats on both sides of it, laughing about something, and his usual friends have shifted down to make room — nobody noticed Theo arrive.

CLUE 1 (face): Theo's smile is on, but his eyebrows are up in the middle — the worried kind of smile.
CLUE 2 (body): He holds the tray tighter and takes one small step back, like the table is a pool he is not sure he may jump into.
CLUE 3 (setting): There IS an empty chair. Nobody has taken his place. But nobody has waved him over, either.

REASON IT OUT (step by step, the detective way): What do we SEE? A smile that does not match, a step backwards, a chair that is free but uninvited. What could he be feeling? Not angry — nothing was taken from him. Not simply happy — happy feet walk forward. The best fit: LEFT OUT, with a sprinkle of nervous.

FEELINGS WORD CARD: LEFT OUT — “the feeling when everyone seems to have a place, and you are not sure yours is still yours.” (Card added to your detective word wallet.)

WHAT WOULD YOU DO? Every scene ends here, with you: A) Wave Theo over and slide down to make room. B) Carry your tray over and sit in the empty chair beside him first. C) Tell a lunch teacher what you noticed. There is no wrong door — but which one would make tomorrow's lunch easier for Theo? Say it in your own words.

### Case · The Wobbly Tower Presentation (complete scene)

THE SCENE — SHOW AND TELL (as played): It is Maya's turn to present the block tower her group built. She carries it to the front on a tray, the way the teacher showed them. Halfway there, the top block slides — not off, just sideways — and lands with a small clack against the tray. Two classmates giggle. It is a short giggle, the surprised kind, and it stops right away. Maya does not look up again.

CLUE 1 (face): Maya's mouth presses into a flat line, and her cheeks go pink — pink that arrives fast, the embarrassed kind.
CLUE 2 (body): She walks the last steps faster, sets the tray down carefully, and stands with her hands held tight together, as if smaller hands might make a smaller target.
CLUE 3 (setting): Nobody broke the tower. The giggle was surprise, not teasing, and it already stopped. But Maya cannot know that yet — from where she stands, the whole class heard it.

SAY IT BACK (the detective way, before any guessing): What do we SEE? A block that slid but did not fall, a short giggle that stopped, pink cheeks, a flat-line mouth, and a walk that sped up. What could she be feeling? Not angry — nobody did anything to her. Not proud, not yet — proud stands taller. The best fit: EMBARRASSED, with a sprinkle of nervous about finishing her talk.

FEELINGS WORD CARD: EMBARRASSED — “the hot-cheek feeling when you think everyone noticed a small mistake more than they really did.” (Card added to your detective word wallet.)

WHAT WOULD YOU DO? A) Look at Maya, smile, and give a small thumbs-up she can see. B) When she finishes, say one true thing you liked about the tower first, before anything else. C) If she looks your way, mouth “it's still standing” — because it is. There is no wrong door — but which one helps Maya believe the giggle was small? Now rehearse it: say the words out loud, the way you would really say them, before the case closes.

DETECTIVE NOTE: Embarrassed is a feeling about what we THINK others noticed. The clues live in the body first — cheeks, mouth, hands — because the feeling arrives before the words do.
