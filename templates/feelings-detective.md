# Classroom Feelings Detective

*Read the clues, guess what a classmate is feeling*

**Category:** Games · **Also listed under:** Education · **Ages:** Ages 5–8 · **Chinese name:** 课堂感受侦探 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

An interactive detective game: each scene offers clues from expressions, body language, and setting, and kids reason step by step about what a classmate is feeling, why, and what they could do. It builds observation, empathy, and feelings vocabulary — wrong guesses are never punished; more clues simply arrive.

## Features

- Clue-based reasoning: expression + action + setting
- Feelings word cards: happy, left out, nervous, angry…
- Wrong guesses add clues — no punishment, no labels
- Each scene ends with: what would you do?
- Parents can see practice history

## Sample content

### Case · The Empty Chair at Lunch (complete scene)

THE SCENE — THE EMPTY CHAIR (as played): It is lunchtime. Theo carries his tray with both hands and stops at the end of the table where he always sits. His chair is there. But today two new kids are sitting in the seats on both sides of it, laughing about something, and his usual friends have shifted down to make room — nobody noticed Theo arrive.

CLUE 1 (face): Theo's smile is on, but his eyebrows are up in the middle — the worried kind of smile.
CLUE 2 (body): He holds the tray tighter and takes one small step back, like the table is a pool he is not sure he may jump into.
CLUE 3 (setting): There IS an empty chair. Nobody has taken his place. But nobody has waved him over, either.

REASON IT OUT (step by step, the detective way): What do we SEE? A smile that does not match, a step backwards, a chair that is free but uninvited. What could he be feeling? Not angry — nothing was taken from him. Not simply happy — happy feet walk forward. The best fit: LEFT OUT, with a sprinkle of nervous.

FEELINGS WORD CARD: LEFT OUT — “the feeling when everyone seems to have a place, and you are not sure yours is still yours.” (Card added to your detective word wallet.)

WHAT WOULD YOU DO? Every scene ends here, with you: A) Wave Theo over and slide down to make room. B) Carry your tray over and sit in the empty chair beside him first. C) Tell a lunch teacher what you noticed. There is no wrong door — but which one would make tomorrow's lunch easier for Theo? Say it in your own words.
