# The Word Roots Garden

*Grow big words from small roots — prefixes, roots and suffixes made visible*

**Category:** Education · **Ages:** Ages 8–11 · **Chinese name:** 词根花园 · **Spec version:** 1.0

## What it is

A word-building studio where big words grow from small parts. Each session tends three garden beds — Prefix Bed, Root Bed and Suffix Bed. Learners meet one new part at a time in a real word, say what the part means, take the word apart and rebuild it, then grow two new words from the same root and use one in their own sentence. Meanings are always checked against how the word is actually used, parts are never guessed from spelling alone, and every session ends with a one-page garden journal: the parts learned, the words grown, and one sentence the learner wrote themselves.

## Sample content

### Sample Lesson · Bed One: See It Before — pre-, re- and the root view (complete worked lesson)

SESSION CARD — Learner: Alex · Ages 8–9 · English · About 20 min · Beds: Prefixes + Roots · Style: Three beds, one part at a time · Helper: Hints first · Journal: One-page garden journal.

THE THREE BEDS — Every big word in this garden grows across three beds.
• PREFIX BED: the part that grows BEFORE the root. It changes when or how the word works.
• ROOT BED: the part in the middle. It carries the core meaning of the word, the way a real root feeds the whole plant.
• SUFFIX BED: the part that grows AFTER the root. It often changes the job of the word — it can turn a doing-word into a person-word, or into an able-to-be word.
Today we tend the Prefix Bed and the Root Bed, one part at a time, always inside a real word. We never guess a part's meaning from spelling alone; we check it against how the word is really used.

BED ONE — THE PREFIX pre- (means BEFORE)
Meet our first word: preview.
preview = pre (before) + view (see) = to see something before the real showing.
Say it in a real sentence: "We watched a preview of the film on Friday, and the real film opens next week."
The preview came first, days before the real film — so the meaning of pre- checks out in the sentence, not just in the spelling.
CHECK: If a preview comes BEFORE, does it come after the film opens? No — a preview is always before the real showing. That is the whole job of pre-.
Counting check: how many letters in preview? p-r-e-v-i-e-w = 7 letters.

BED TWO — THE PREFIX re- (means AGAIN / BACK)
Same root, new prefix: review.
review = re (again) + view (see) = to look again — to see a thing one more time, on purpose.
And one more, grown on a different root: rebuild.
rebuild = re (again) + build (make) = to make again — when a tower of blocks falls down, you rebuild it.
Notice how re- keeps its meaning (again) in both words, even though the roots are different. That is how a word gardener learns to trust a part: it does the same job in word after word.

ROOT OF THE DAY — view / vis (means SEE)
One root can grow a whole family. Our root is view, which also appears in its older shape vis — and both mean SEE. Grow four words from it:
1. preview — to see before. "The class watched a preview of the school play before opening night."
2. review — to see again. "Before the quiz, we review our notes so we can see them again."
3. viewer — a person who watches. The suffix -er means a person who does the thing. "Every viewer clapped when the kite finally flew."
4. visible — able to be seen. The suffix -ible means able to be. "From the top of the hill, the whole harbour was visible."
Four words, one root, and one meaning at the centre of all of them: SEE.

BUILD-A-WORD WORKSHOP — you be the gardener (answers given)
1. pre + heat = preheat — to heat before cooking. "Preheat the oven before the muffins go in."
2. re + read = reread — to read again. "A favourite book always deserves a reread."
3. un + lock = unlock — the prefix un- means NOT, or reverses the action: to open something that was locked. "Turn the key to unlock the garden gate."

SORT IT — which prefix bed does each word belong in?
preview → pre- · review → re- · unlock → un- · preheat → pre-
All four sorted: two pre- words, one re- word, one un- word.

MY OWN SENTENCE — Alex's turn to close the session
Alex writes: "I watched a preview of the puppet show, and now I cannot wait to see the real one."
The word preview is used correctly: something seen before the real showing.

GARDEN JOURNAL — today's page
Parts learned: pre- = before · re- = again · un- = reverse / not · -er = a person who does · -ible = able to be · root view / vis = see.
Words grown: preview, review, viewer, visible, preheat, reread, unlock — count them: 7 words, all grown in today's beds.
The garden rule to keep: take a word apart, name each part, then check the meaning in a real sentence — and a big word stops being a stranger. It becomes a plant you grew yourself.
